# Sales Plan

> **Kidd's Technical Services, LLC**
> Built on trust. Grounded in what's real.

## Purpose

This document is the operating sales plan for Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS).

The Go-to-Market Plan explains *who* KTS serves and *why*. The Local Business Outreach Playbook explains *how* to run one customer through the process. The Revenue Model and Financial Projections explain *what the money needs to look like*. The Local Business Sales Ramp sets *how many packages per month* the cash-flow plan assumes.

This Sales Plan connects those documents. It answers one practical question:

> How much sales activity does KTS need each week to sell the number of packages the ramp assumes — sized to a solo, founder-led, caregiver-phased business with $0 starting cash?

It converts the ramp's monthly package targets into a weekly funnel of contacts, discovery conversations, and proposals, so the founder can look at one number each week and know whether KTS is on pace.

This is a planning document. It is not a funding approval, customer contract, revenue guarantee, or promise of results controlled by any third party. The conversion rates below are **planning assumptions to be replaced with actual pipeline evidence** as real numbers come in.

## Current Sales Position

KTS states its starting point plainly, the same way the WVSBDC meeting brief does:

- KTS is pre-revenue and bootstrapped. Starting business cash is $0.
- KTS has no current contracts, deposits, invoices, purchase orders, or letters of intent.
- Sales and delivery are founder-led. Founder payroll is $0 in the starter stage.
- Contractor work is project-funded only. No standing contractor commitment exists.
- The founder is also a caregiver. Sales activity must be phased realistically around that responsibility, not planned as if a full-time salesperson were available.
- ALLIS is not a current offering and produces $0 revenue. It is excluded from this plan.

This is not a weakness to hide. It is the reason the plan below is built around a **small, steady weekly rhythm** rather than a large sales push KTS cannot sustain.

### An honest note on average price

The sales ramp assumes an **average booked package of $2,500**. The entry package is $500. These two facts only reconcile if most booked revenue comes from the $1,500–$2,500 tiers and the occasional larger map, heritage, or partner project — not from a stack of $500 cleanups.

The $500 package is a way to *start a relationship*, not the plan for hitting the average. An illustrative mix that averages $2,500 across twelve sales:

## The sales funnel

To turn "packages per month" into "activity per week," KTS uses a simple four-stage funnel with conversion assumptions.

```text
Qualified contact  (a real prospect, reached, with a plausible need)
        ↓  30%
Discovery conversation  (a real 20–30 min conversation about their problem)
        ↓  60%
Proposal sent  (written scope, price, deposit terms)
        ↓  35%
Closed – deposit collected  (a sale)
```

**Net conversion, contact → sale:** 0.30 × 0.60 × 0.35 ≈ **6.3%**, or roughly **one sale for every 16 qualified contacts.**

> These three percentages are planning assumptions, chosen on the conservative side for mostly-cold local outreach with warm community ties. They are the single most important thing to replace with real numbers. After the first ~20–30 contacts, recalculate each rate from the Pipeline Tracker and rebuild the activity targets below. Real data beats this table every time.

## Activity targets

Applying the funnel to the ramp gives the activity KTS must generate. Because a contact closes 1–2 months after it is made, **outreach in a given month is aimed at the target one to two months ahead.**

| Ramp stage | Sales / mo | Proposals / mo | Discoveries / mo | Qualified contacts / mo | Contacts / **week** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dec 2026 – Feb 2027 | 1 | ~3 | ~5 | ~16 | **~4** |
| Mar – Aug 2027 | 2 | ~6 | ~10 | ~32 | **~8** |
| Sep 2027 – Feb 2028 | 3 | ~9 | ~15 | ~48 | **~12** |
| Mar – Aug 2028 | 4 | ~11 | ~19 | ~64 | **~16** |

The headline for a caregiver-phased founder: **the first year asks for roughly 4 to 8 real prospect contacts per week.** That is achievable in two focused blocks a week. The plan does not require a full-time seller until volume is well past the starter stage — and by then, collected revenue can fund help.

## Weekly sales cadence

The plan lives or dies on a repeatable weekly rhythm, not on bursts. Sized to limited hours:

| Block | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning block** (start of week) | ~30 min | Review pipeline, this week's follow-ups due, proposals outstanding, deposits expected. Set this week's contact target from the table above. |
| **Outreach block 1** | ~60–90 min | Research 3–5 new prospects; make first contacts; send the week's follow-ups. |
| **Outreach block 2** | ~60–90 min | Hold scheduled discovery conversations; write and send any proposals that are ready. |
| **Delivery** (as sold) | Varies | Only after deposit is collected. Kept separate from selling time. |

Two to three hours of *selling* a week covers the first-year targets. Delivery time is additional and only exists once there is a paid deposit to justify it — which protects the founder from doing unpaid work.

## First 90 days: the sprint to the first sale

This aligns the ramp's December first sale with the Bootstrap Launch Plan's 90-day structure. Assuming a start around October 2026:

**Days 1–30 (≈ October) — Load the pipeline.**
- Build the first list of 25–50 local prospects using the Playbook's prospect record (public info only).
- Confirm the intake form, proposal template, invoice template, and a working copy of the Pipeline Tracker.
- Make **~16–20 first contacts** across the month (~4–5/week). Goal: fill the funnel, not close.
- Book the first discovery conversations.

**Days 31–60 (≈ November) — Convert to proposals.**
- Run **~5–8 discovery conversations.**
- Send **~3–5 real proposals** with scope, price, deposit terms, and an expiration date.
- Recalculate the three conversion rates from actual results and update the activity targets.

**Days 61–90 (≈ December) — Close and deliver the first one.**
- Collect the first **deposit** and start the first paid project.
- Deliver, get written acceptance, collect the final payment.
- Ask for the first referral and, with permission, the first case study or before/after screenshot.
- This single documented, paid, accepted project is the most valuable sales asset KTS can own — it replaces assumptions with evidence for every lender, funder, and next customer.

## Weekly scorecard

Track these leading indicators every week in the Pipeline Tracker. They show whether KTS is on pace *before* the revenue arrives.

| Metric | Why it matters | This week's target (Dec 2026–Feb 2027 stage) |
|---|---|---:|
| New qualified contacts made | The top of the funnel; the earliest warning | ~4 |
| Discovery conversations held | Interest and message quality | ~1–2 |
| Proposals sent | Real qualified opportunities | ~1 every 1–2 weeks |
| Deposits collected | The only number that is actually revenue | per ramp |
| Follow-ups due and done | Most sales are lost to silence, not "no" | all of them |

If contacts fall behind, everything downstream falls behind a month later. Protect the top of the funnel first.

## When to adjust

The plan is a starting hypothesis. Change it when the evidence says to.

| What the real numbers show | What to do |
|---|---|
| Close rate **below** the assumed 6.3% | Increase weekly contacts to hold the ramp, *and* examine which stage is leaking — usually discovery→proposal (wrong-fit prospects) or proposal→close (price or scope mismatch). |
| Close rate **above** assumption | Hold contacts steady and let the pipeline run ahead; or keep pace and build a small backlog cushion. |
| Average sale **below** $2,500 | Shift outreach toward the $1,500–$2,500 tiers and the partner/heritage/grant-map lines; the $500 tier alone will not carry the ramp. |
| One segment converts far better | Concentrate outreach there; document why for the next SBDC review. |
| Delivery time exceeds the sale's value | Reprice that package or tighten scope before selling more of it. |

Recalculate at least monthly against actual Pipeline Tracker data, exactly as the Revenue Model's update process requires.

## Diversify beyond one-off local jobs

Local-business packages generate cash, referrals, and portfolio proof, but they are small. To reduce dependence on a stream of individual small sales — a risk the Financial Projections name explicitly — work at least one higher-value channel in parallel from the start:

- **One partner firm relationship.** A single web or marketing firm that sends recurring map/GIS subcontract work is worth many cold local contacts. Approach with the one-line capability statement in the Go-to-Market Plan.
- **One grant-ready map customer.** Nonprofits and towns need project maps on grant cycles; these are repeatable and often lead to larger public-information work.
- **One heritage or genealogy discovery project.** These match the founder's strengths and open the door to larger preservation and access engagements.

One live opportunity in each of these three channels, plus the local-business rhythm, is a materially more durable pipeline than local business alone.

## Boundaries

This plan operates inside the same boundaries as every other KTS document:

- No promise of Google ranking, verification, traffic, sales, advertising results, grant awards, or any third-party outcome.
- No prospect, conversation, verbal interest, or grant possibility is counted as revenue. Only a collected deposit is.
- Deposit and payment terms are followed before contractor-heavy or material work begins.
- The smallest useful scope is offered first; no customer is pushed into a larger package than they need.
- ALLIS, Harmony for Hope, and MountainShares are not treated as automatic customer-acquisition channels or funding sources for KTS.
- ALLIS produces $0 sales revenue in this plan and is excluded from every target above.
- Planning assumptions are replaced with actual evidence as it accumulates.

## Operating principle

> KTS earns revenue by making a steady, honest number of real contacts each week, understanding a genuine customer problem, offering the smallest useful scope, collecting a deposit, delivering well, and turning each finished project into the proof that wins the next one.

The goal of this plan is not a big launch. It is a rhythm small enough to keep every week and reliable enough to compound — four to eight real conversations a week, one paid project at a time, until the pipeline and the evidence justify hiring the help to do more.
