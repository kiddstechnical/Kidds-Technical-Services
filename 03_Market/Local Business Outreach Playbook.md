# Local Business Outreach Playbook

> **Kidd’s Technical Services, LLC**  
> Trusted Local Information  
> Built on trust. Grounded in what’s real.

## Purpose

This playbook defines KTS’s repeatable workflow for finding local-business customers, understanding their real information problems, offering the smallest useful service scope, collecting payment, delivering approved work, and creating a path to referrals and ongoing Local Information Care.

KTS is not selling generic “marketing,” rankings, AI optimization, or attention. KTS helps local businesses maintain clear, current, business-approved information that customers can find, understand, and use.

The core customer problem is simple:

> Customers increasingly ask search engines, maps, social platforms, and AI tools where a business is, when it is open, what it offers, how to reach it, and whether the information is current. The answers they receive can be incomplete, stale, confusing, or wrong.

KTS helps businesses establish and maintain an accountable source of truth for their location, hours, services, directions, access details, events, contact information, and other approved customer information.

This is an internal operating guide. It is not a guarantee of customer acquisition, revenue, third-party platform performance, search ranking, AI-search visibility, business results, or service availability.

## Operating Principle

> Start with what customers need to know. Verify what is true. Publish only what the business approves. Maintain it when reality changes.

KTS should recommend the smallest useful scope first. Do not force a larger package, ongoing plan, technical platform, map product, contractor engagement, travel requirement, or subscription onto a customer who does not need it.

## Customer Journey

```text
Build local prospect list
        ↓
Research public information and identify a possible issue
        ↓
Make respectful first contact
        ↓
Hold a short discovery conversation
        ↓
Identify the customer-information problem and desired outcome
        ↓
Recommend the smallest useful Trust Path scope
        ↓
Send written proposal with scope, exclusions, price, deposit, timeline, and approvals
        ↓
Receive approval and required payment or deposit
        ↓
Verify information and deliver approved work
        ↓
Receive customer acceptance and final payment
        ↓
Request permission for referral, review, case study, or introduction
        ↓
Offer Keep Current: Ongoing Local Information Care when appropriate
```

## Ideal Early Customers

Prioritize businesses and organizations where location, hours, access, directions, pickup, events, or public information directly affect customer experience.

Examples include:

- Restaurants, cafes, food trucks, bakeries, and caterers
- Retail stores, antique stores, repair shops, and local makers
- Lodging, vacation rentals, campgrounds, outfitters, and tourism businesses
- Event venues, museums, arts organizations, theaters, and community spaces
- Health, wellness, fitness, and personal-service businesses
- Contractors, home-service businesses, and mobile-service providers
- Auto repair, towing, delivery, transportation, and roadside businesses
- Farms, markets, roadside stands, and seasonal businesses
- Churches, nonprofits, civic groups, and community events
- Businesses with multiple entrances, parking instructions, pickup areas, accessibility information, rural locations, or changing hours

A good early prospect has a visible information problem, a reachable decision-maker, a practical need, and enough potential value to justify a small paid project.

## Prospect List

Build a working list of 25–50 local prospects before beginning broad outreach.

Use only public, permissioned, or customer-provided information. Do not scrape restricted sources, misrepresent KTS, impersonate a customer, create accounts for a prospect without authorization, or collect unnecessary personal information.

### Prospect Record

For each prospect, track:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Business name | Official public-facing name |
| Business type | Restaurant, venue, contractor, retailer, nonprofit, and so on |
| Primary location or service area | General location; avoid unnecessary private details |
| Public website | Website URL, if available |
| Public contact method | Phone, public email, contact form, or social account |
| Decision-maker or contact | Name and role only if publicly provided or learned with permission |
| Information issue observed | Outdated hours, unclear entrance, missing parking instructions, inconsistent contact details, confusing service area, missing event details, and so on |
| Customer impact | What a customer may be unable to understand, find, confirm, or do |
| Possible KTS starting scope | Verify, Publish, Guide, or discovery only |
| Outreach date | Date of first contact |
| Outreach method | Walk-in, referral, phone, email, event, social message, or introduction |
| Response status | No response, conversation scheduled, not now, not a fit, proposal requested, proposal sent, won, lost, follow-up date |
| Next action | Specific next step and date |
| Notes | Factual, respectful notes only; no sensitive speculation |

### Prospecting Sources

Use:

- Direct observation of customer-access or public-information problems
- Local business directories and chambers of commerce
- Downtown, tourism, main-street, and community-event listings
- Referrals from existing customers and trusted local contacts
- Public websites, public maps, and public business listings
- Community events, vendor markets, networking meetings, and business gatherings
- Local organizations that already support small businesses

Do not lead with criticism of a business’s current information. Lead with the customer experience and an offer to help.

## Research Before Contact

Spend no more than 10–15 minutes on a first-pass review unless the prospect has requested more.

Check only public information relevant to customer access:

- Business name, public phone number, website, and primary contact method
- Listed hours and whether hours agree across the business’s own public channels
- Address, pin location, service area, parking, pickup, entrance, or accessibility details when relevant
- Whether a first-time customer could understand where to go and what to do
- Whether a customer can find a clear, business-approved source for current information
- Whether a special event, seasonal schedule, closure, relocation, renovation, or service change may create confusion
- Whether there is a likely short-term opportunity for KTS to help

Record facts, not assumptions. Do not tell a prospect that a platform, directory, or AI answer is “wrong” unless the business confirms it.

## First Contact

The purpose of first contact is to earn a short conversation—not to sell a package immediately.

## Discovery Conversation

Keep the first discovery conversation to 20–30 minutes unless the customer requests more time.

The goal is to understand the customer’s real situation, not to demonstrate every KTS capability.

### Discovery Questions

Ask:

1. What do customers most often ask before they arrive, call, book, buy, or attend?
2. What information do customers seem to get wrong, miss, or find confusing?
3. Where do customers currently look for information about your business?
4. Are your hours, services, location, phone number, website, and directions consistent across your business-controlled public channels?
5. Do customers need parking, entrance, pickup, accessibility, venue, service-area, event, or seasonal information?
6. Has anything recently changed or is anything about to change?
7. Who can confirm what is accurate and approve public information?
8. What systems, accounts, website pages, maps, QR codes, or materials does the business currently control?
9. What would make the situation better for customers and staff?
10. Is there a deadline, event, season, opening, move, promotion, or operational change driving the need?
11. What budget range, if any, is the business comfortable considering?
12. Would a one-time verified update be enough, or does the business expect information to change regularly?

### Discovery Notes

Record:

- The customer’s own words about the problem
- The customer’s preferred outcome
- Verified facts and open questions
- The identified authorized approver
- Required access, permissions, materials, deadlines, and dependencies
- What KTS should not change, publish, access, or assume
- Any likely scope boundary or risk requiring follow-up

Do not collect passwords or sensitive credentials during discovery. Use a documented, approved transfer method only after a signed scope requires access.

## Recommend the Smallest Useful Scope

Match the offer to the identified customer problem.

| Customer need | Recommended starting scope | Customer result |
|---|---|---|
| Core details are missing, inconsistent, outdated, or confusing | **Verify — Verified Local Presence** | A business-approved foundation of accurate, organized customer information |
| Customers need one clear public place for information | **Publish — Trusted Information Hub** | A mobile-friendly, business-approved source for directions, services, access, contact, or event details |
| Customers have trouble arriving, parking, entering, picking up, or understanding a location or service area | **Guide — Local Access Map** | Clear customer-facing location, access, parking, entrance, pickup, event, or service-area guidance |
| Information changes often after the initial project | **Keep Current — Ongoing Local Information Care** | A defined review, approval, update, and change-record process |

Use simple language:

```text
Based on what you described, I recommend we start with [package]. It addresses [customer problem] by providing [plain-language outcome]. The starting price is [price], and the expected timeline is [timeline] once we have the information, permissions, and approvals we need.
```

Do not promise results outside KTS control. KTS does not promise a particular Google ranking, verification, traffic level, sales result, advertising result, AI-search result, or third-party display outcome.

## Proposal Workflow

Send a written proposal only after you can describe the problem, scope, price, timeline, customer responsibilities, and approval process clearly.

Every proposal must include:

- Customer and project identification
- The customer problem and intended outcome
- Detailed scope and deliverables
- Exclusions and third-party boundaries
- Price, deposit, payment schedule, and payment method
- Timeline, dependencies, and customer-review periods
- Customer responsibilities, access needs, and approvals
- Revision limits and change-control process
- Acceptance criteria and handoff process
- Data, credential, confidentiality, and storage expectations where applicable
- Ownership or licensing terms, if applicable
- Proposal expiration date
- KTS contact information

Use `06_agreements/Small_Project_Proposal_Checklist.md` before sending a proposal.

### Deposit Rule

For most projects over $1,000:

```text
50% deposit before work begins
50% before launch or handoff
```

Small projects may require full payment in advance.

Do not begin material work, make nonrefundable purchases, authorize contractors, travel, or publish customer information before receiving the required approval and payment under the proposal.

## Follow-Up Workflow

Follow up professionally and without pressure.

### After First Contact

- If no response, send one follow-up after 5–7 business days.
- If still no response, send one final short follow-up after another 7–14 business days.
- If there is no response after that, mark the prospect as inactive and do not continue unsolicited follow-up unless circumstances change or the prospect re-engages.

### After Discovery

- Send a short recap within 1–2 business days.
- Confirm the customer’s stated problem, desired outcome, agreed next step, and any materials KTS needs.
- Send the proposal when scope is clear.
- If a proposal is not ready, state what information or decision is needed before KTS can prepare it.

### After Proposal

- Confirm receipt within 1–2 business days if needed.
- Send one follow-up before the proposal expires.
- Ask a specific, low-pressure question:

```text
Would you like to move forward, revise the scope, schedule a short call, or pause the project for now?
```

- Do not reduce price, expand scope, promise third-party results, or add unpaid work solely to overcome hesitation.
- If the customer declines, record the reason if offered and thank them.

## Deposit and Project Start

Before work begins, confirm:

- The proposal is accepted by an authorized customer representative
- The required deposit or advance payment is received and recorded
- The customer has provided required materials, access, and permissions
- The customer has identified the authorized approver
- The project folder has been created
- The schedule and customer review dates are confirmed
- Any third-party, travel, contractor, printing, platform, insurance, or equipment cost is approved
- Any scope uncertainty is resolved or documented as a change-control item

Create a project folder using the KTS operating-stack structure:

```text
Customer_Name/
├── 01_Proposal_and_Agreement/
├── 02_Customer_Provided_Materials/
├── 03_Working_Files/
├── 04_Approvals_and_Change_Requests/
├── 05_Final_Deliverables/
├── 06_Invoices_and_Payment_Records/
└── 07_Handoff_and_Closeout/
```

## Delivery Workflow

### 1. Verify

- Collect only the information needed for the approved scope
- Organize business-provided information
- Identify conflicts, missing details, unclear instructions, or open questions
- Ask the customer to confirm facts before public use
- Record what information was approved, by whom, and when
- Do not represent information as verified if the business has not approved it

### 2. Build

- Create the agreed deliverables
- Use customer-approved text, locations, logos, photos, links, and other materials
- Keep working materials separate from public deliverables
- Follow relevant platform terms, licensing, privacy, accessibility, attribution, and data-use requirements
- Do not place customer credentials, private data, API keys, passwords, or tokens in GitHub or other public locations

### 3. Review and Approve

- Send a clear draft or preview to the authorized approver
- Identify exactly what the customer is approving
- Provide a reasonable review deadline
- Record approval in the project folder
- Treat requests outside scope as change requests
- Do not publish public information without the required approval

### 4. Launch and Handoff

- Publish, deliver, or transfer only the materials included in scope
- Confirm links, QR codes, maps, contact information, and customer-access information work as intended
- Provide any included handoff materials, files, instructions, or access information
- Record the final delivery date
- Send the final invoice according to the approved payment terms
- Do not transfer final ownership, source files, administrative access, or final deliverables before payment if the proposal makes payment a condition of handoff

## Change Control

A request is a change request if it adds, removes, expands, or materially alters an approved deliverable, location, platform, map, event, review cycle, deadline, travel requirement, account-access need, customer group, or level of effort.

When a change is requested:

1. Record the request in writing.
2. Determine the effect on price, timeline, deliverables, risks, dependencies, and payment.
3. Send written change terms.
4. Obtain approval from the authorized customer representative.
5. Receive any required additional deposit or payment.
6. Update the project folder and schedule.
7. Begin the changed work only after approval.

Verbal requests, text messages, informal conversations, or assumptions do not change scope unless KTS confirms the change in writing.

## Acceptance and Final Payment

Before closing a project:

- Confirm the approved deliverables are complete
- Give the customer the agreed review opportunity
- Correct material issues within the approved scope
- Document customer acceptance or approval
- Issue and collect the final invoice
- Deliver or transfer final materials according to the approved terms
- Confirm what KTS will retain, delete, return, or archive
- Record the project’s actual time, direct costs, contractor costs, travel, tools used, revenue, and gross-margin estimate
- Identify lessons that should change pricing, scope, templates, workflow, or future service offers

## Referral and Review Request

Ask only after the customer has received value and the project is complete.

### Referral Request

```text
I’m glad we were able to help make your information clearer for customers.

If you know another local business, venue, organization, or event that has trouble with location details, hours, access, directions, pickup, service-area information, or keeping public information current, I would appreciate an introduction.
```

### Review or Testimonial Request

```text
If you are comfortable doing so, would you be willing to share a short review of what KTS helped clarify and what changed for your customers or staff?

KTS will ask for your permission before using any quote, logo, map, photo, or project detail publicly.
```

### Case Study Permission

```text
Would you be open to KTS preparing a short case study that explains the customer-information problem, the approved solution, and the outcome? We will send it to you for review and will not publish names, images, details, or results without your written permission.
```

Do not offer incentives for reviews in a way that violates platform rules. Do not draft or post a review on a customer’s behalf.

## Keep Current: Ongoing Local Information Care

Offer ongoing care when the customer has information that changes regularly or when a one-time project creates a trusted information source that needs maintenance.

Good fit indicators:

- Seasonal, holiday, or variable hours
- Recurring events
- New services, menus, offers, staff contacts, or service-area changes
- Relocation, renovation, construction, parking changes, or new entrances
- Pickup, delivery, accessibility, or access instructions that change
- Multiple locations, programs, venues, or public-information channels
- A customer who wants a defined process rather than emergency updates

### Care-Plan Discovery

Before quoting ongoing care, define:

- Review frequency: monthly, quarterly, seasonal, event-based, or another agreed cadence
- Covered locations, pages, platforms, QR destinations, maps, and information types
- Customer responsibilities and authorized approver
- Included update volume or time allowance
- Response timeframe for review and approval
- What is excluded
- Additional-work rate or change process
- Start date, renewal terms, pause or cancellation terms, and payment schedule
- Recordkeeping, access, and handoff expectations

### Care-Plan Promise

Use this language:

```text
KTS helps maintain the business-approved source named in the care-plan scope. KTS does not control third-party display, indexing, ranking, directory accuracy, social-platform distribution, or AI-generated summaries.
```

Do not call a plan “unlimited,” “fully managed everywhere,” or “guaranteed accurate across the internet.”

## Pipeline Metrics

Review the outreach pipeline weekly during the bootstrap stage.

Track:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| New prospects added | Shows whether KTS has enough outreach activity |
| First contacts made | Measures active outreach |
| Discovery conversations completed | Measures prospect interest and message quality |
| Proposals sent | Measures qualified opportunities |
| Proposal acceptance rate | Shows whether offer, price, scope, and timing fit the market |
| Deposits collected | Shows whether KTS has real paid work to begin |
| Projects completed | Shows delivery capacity and customer value |
| Final payments collected | Shows cash-flow reliability |
| Average project value | Helps evaluate service pricing and offer mix |
| Direct project cost and delivery time | Helps calculate actual margin and pricing needs |
| Referrals, reviews, and introductions received | Indicates trust and customer satisfaction |
| Care-plan offers and conversions | Indicates recurring-service potential |
| Reasons for lost or delayed work | Helps refine targeting, outreach, pricing, and scope |

Do not treat unverified interest, verbal intent, social-media engagement, grant possibilities, or speculative opportunities as revenue.

## Conduct and Trust Boundaries

KTS will:

- Be respectful, local, direct, and honest
- Describe observed public-information issues factually and without embarrassment or pressure
- Ask permission before accessing accounts, property, data, logos, photos, maps, customer records, or private information
- Publish only business-approved public information
- Keep customer working files, credentials, and private information secure and separate from public materials
- Use written scope, approval, payment, and change-control processes
- Acknowledge what KTS does not control
- Refer customers to qualified professionals when work requires legal, tax, surveying, engineering, insurance, accessibility, emergency-management, or other regulated expertise

KTS will not:

- Claim to control Google, AI systems, search rankings, maps, directories, social platforms, or customer outcomes
- Create, alter, claim, verify, or take control of a customer account without documented authorization
- Use fear-based claims about AI, misinformation, or online risk
- Promise that every third-party system will display customer information correctly
- Use unapproved customer information or publish material without the required approval
- Begin material work before required approval and payment
- Represent customer information as verified when it has not been approved by the customer
- Continue outreach after a prospect has clearly declined contact

## Success Standard

The outreach workflow succeeds when KTS can repeatedly demonstrate:

```text
Respectful outreach
    ↓
Clear understanding of a real customer-information problem
    ↓
Small, written, customer-approved scope
    ↓
Required deposit and documented project start
    ↓
Accurate, approved delivery
    ↓
Customer acceptance and final payment
    ↓
Referral, review, care plan, or follow-on work
    ↓
Measured learning that improves the next customer cycle
```

The objective is not to pressure businesses into a package. The objective is to become a trusted local partner that helps people, places, and communities keep important information clear, current, and accountable.
