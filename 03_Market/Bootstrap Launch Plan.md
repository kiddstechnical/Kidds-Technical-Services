# Bootstrap Launch Plan

> **Built on trust. Grounded in what’s real.**

## Purpose

This document describes Kidd’s Technical Services, LLC (KTS)’s immediate, lean launch plan.

KTS is bootstrapping. The purpose of the first stage is not to build a fully staffed organization before customer demand exists. The purpose is to use available tools, clear service offers, direct local outreach, customer deposits, careful delivery, and collected revenue to establish a repeatable business.

KTS will begin with practical local-business services that can be delivered with existing skills and low-cost tools. KTS will add equipment, software, contractors, payroll, managed-service obligations, and other operating costs only when customer demand, paid work, collected revenue, or approved working capital supports them.

This is a planning document. It is not a funding approval, customer contract, revenue guarantee, employment offer, contractor commitment, or promise of service availability.

## Bootstrap Boundary

KTS begins with a lean operating model.

| Item | Bootstrap treatment |
|---|---|
| Founder labor | Founder-led delivery and sales; no founder payroll assumed during the starter stage |
| Founder compensation target | $100,000 annual target for a later sustainable stage; not assumed as current payroll |
| Website | Existing KTS web presence; no new website-development cost assumed |
| KTS domain | Estimated $20 annually |
| Business email | Existing SiteGround-hosted email capacity, subject to an appropriate KTS arrangement |
| Mapping software | QGIS at $0 initial cost |
| Storage | Existing available capacity first; add paid storage only when an actual business or customer need requires it |
| Accounting and invoicing | Use the lowest suitable QuickBooks plan when KTS begins invoicing; confirm actual price before purchase |
| Contractors | Project-funded only; no standing weekly-hour or annual contractor commitment during the starter stage |
| Employees | No employee payroll until sustained collections, payroll review, compliance setup, and available cash support it |
| ALLIS | $0 current revenue; no ALLIS revenue, product commercialization, staffing, or repayment assumption in this launch plan |
| Harmony for Hope and MountainShares | Separate organizations; their funds, domains, platform costs, data, and obligations are not KTS expenses unless a written agreement authorizes a specific KTS service or reimbursement |

KTS does not use personal funds, Harmony for Hope funds, MountainShares funds, donor funds, grant funds, customer funds, contractor funds, or KTS business funds interchangeably. Each entity and funding source must remain separately documented and authorized.

## Immediate Service Focus

KTS will begin by selling local-business packages that solve immediate customer problems.

| Initial service | Customer result | Typical payment protection |
|---|---|---|
| Get Found Locally package | Accurate business information, location, hours, services, photos, and directions | Full payment in advance or 50% deposit |
| Google Business Profile setup or cleanup | Customer-controlled business profile with corrected information and map placement | Full payment in advance or 50% deposit |
| Find Us, parking, pickup, or service-area page | Clear mobile-friendly directions and customer-access information | 50% deposit; balance before launch |
| QR-linked information hub | One simple destination for services, menus, directions, events, booking, or contact details | 50% deposit; balance before launch |
| Event, venue, delivery, or service-area map | Clear location information for customers, visitors, vendors, or delivery clients | 50% deposit; milestone billing if needed |
| Local listing care plan | Scheduled updates for hours, services, directions, photos, and business information | Paid monthly, quarterly, or annually in advance |

KTS does not promise Google ranking, Google verification, customer traffic, customer sales, advertising results, or any result controlled by a third party.

## First Customer Cycle

```text
Identify a local business with a clear information, location,
directions, service-area, or customer-access problem
        ↓
Conduct a short discovery conversation
        ↓
Define a small fixed-price scope and customer outcome
        ↓
Send a written proposal with price, timeline, and deposit requirement
        ↓
Receive signed approval and required payment or deposit
        ↓
Deliver work using existing tools and documented customer approvals
        ↓
Collect final payment before launch or handoff, as agreed
        ↓
Request customer approval for a case study, referral, review,
care plan, or follow-on work
```

KTS will not begin contractor-heavy, travel-heavy, equipment-heavy, or vendor-heavy work until the customer pays the required deposit or another authorized funding pathway exists.

## First 90 Days

### Days 1–30: Establish and prepare

KTS will:

- Confirm KTS’s business formation, banking, records, invoicing, and bookkeeping process.
- Purchase or register the KTS domain if needed.
- Set up the minimum required invoicing and expense-tracking process.
- Obtain actual quotes for insurance, any essential equipment, accounting, and any required professional support.
- Prepare a one-page local-business offer sheet.
- Prepare a proposal/estimate template, invoice template, customer intake form, and service checklist.
- Identify 25–50 potential local-business customers.
- Begin direct outreach, relationship-building, discovery conversations, and referral requests.
- Create a pipeline record for every lead, conversation, proposal, deposit, invoice, collection, and customer outcome.

### Days 31–60: Sell and collect deposits

KTS will:

- Complete customer-discovery conversations.
- Send a limited number of real proposals with clear scope, price, deposit terms, and expiration dates.
- Prioritize smaller fixed-price work that can be delivered with existing tools.
- Collect deposits before beginning material work.
- Use customer deposits and approved working capital only for authorized delivery costs.
- Track which outreach activity produces meetings, proposals, deposits, and paid work.
- Avoid paid advertising or major equipment purchases unless a documented business need supports the cost.

### Days 61–90: Deliver, collect, and repeat

KTS will:

- Deliver the first paid projects with clear customer review and acceptance.
- Collect final payments under the agreed terms.
- Record direct project costs, delivery time, expenses, gross margin, and customer feedback.
- Request permission to create a case study or use a testimonial, where appropriate.
- Offer an update plan, care plan, map maintenance, support, or follow-on service when it meets a real customer need.
- Use collected revenue to support the next customer cycle.
- Update the pipeline, bookkeeping records, and cash-flow plan using actual results.

## Working-Capital Discipline

KTS may seek or receive startup funding, but funding does not create permission to spend without control.

For a potential $25,000 or $50,000 startup funding case, KTS will use capital only for documented, lender-authorized, and business-necessary purposes. KTS will prioritize:

1. Required business formation, insurance, compliance, and financial-record systems.
2. Essential equipment or tools KTS cannot deliver paid work without.
3. KTS domain, accounting, invoicing, secure storage, and necessary business operations.
4. Direct local-business customer acquisition and proposal development.
5. Project-specific delivery costs after a signed scope and customer deposit.
6. A controlled working-capital reserve for ordinary startup needs, customer-payment timing gaps, and limited project-funded staffing support.

KTS will not use startup funding for:

- Personal expenses, caregiver expenses, or personal debt.
- Harmony for Hope, MountainShares, donor, grant, customer, or other entity expenses without written authorization.
- Founder salary during the starter stage.
- Standing contractor commitments without customer funding or documented available working capital.
- ALLIS product development, commercialization, deployment, subscription claims, or assumed revenue.
- A new website build, paid GIS software, or speculative technology purchases that KTS does not need to deliver initial paid services.
- Untracked, undocumented, or non-business spending.

## Staffing Rules

KTS will build capacity only as demand supports it.

| Business condition | KTS action |
|---|---|
| No paid customer work | Founder-led outreach and delivery preparation; no hiring commitment |
| Signed scope and customer deposit | Authorize defined contractor work only within the funded project budget |
| Multiple paid projects with reliable collections | Reserve limited contractor capacity for specific delivery needs |
| Sustained collections, positive project margins, and sufficient working capital | Consider a regular contractor relationship or part-time employee role after payroll, insurance, HR, tax, classification, and cash-flow review |
| Specialized need tied to a paid customer project | Use a written contractor scope, rate, not-to-exceed amount, confidentiality terms, and invoice process |
| Revenue delay or project cancellation | Pause nonessential spending, contractor expansion, and staffing commitments |

KTS will not hire or promise compensation solely because funding is received. Staffing decisions require a documented business need, a defined role, available cash, and an appropriate written arrangement.

## Financial Triggers

KTS will review these indicators monthly:

| Indicator | Bootstrap decision use |
|---|---|
| Customer deposits collected | Determines whether KTS can begin direct delivery work |
| Final payments collected | Determines whether KTS can retain cash and repeat delivery |
| Direct project cost | Determines actual gross margin and whether pricing must change |
| Cash on hand | Determines whether KTS can make essential purchases or authorize work |
| Pipeline value with evidence | Determines whether near-term work is credible enough to plan capacity |
| Proposal conversion | Determines whether the offer, price, customer segment, or outreach approach needs adjustment |
| Repeat customers, care plans, and referrals | Indicates whether KTS is developing a durable business rather than isolated one-time work |
| Actual operating expense | Replaces planning assumptions with real costs |
| Contractor cost and time | Determines whether work should remain founder-led, be contracted, or be repriced |

KTS will update its cash-flow plan, revenue assumptions, and use-of-funds plan whenever actual results show that a planning assumption is inaccurate.

## Success Measures

The bootstrap launch succeeds when KTS can demonstrate:

- A clear, lawful, customer-ready local-business offer.
- A documented prospect list and regular outreach process.
- Real customer discovery conversations.
- Written proposals with clear scope, price, deposit terms, and customer responsibilities.
- At least one paid project using documented customer approval and payment.
- Accurate invoices, expense records, customer-payment records, and project-cost records.
- A completed customer outcome, with permission-based case-study or referral evidence where appropriate.
- A repeatable process for deposits, delivery, final payment, and follow-on support.
- Financial evidence that can replace assumptions in future lender, grant, partner, or growth conversations.

## Operating Principle

> KTS will earn the right to grow by solving real customer problems, collecting payment, delivering well, documenting results, and protecting trust.

The bootstrap stage is intentionally disciplined. KTS will use existing capability first, spend only when a real business need exists, keep commitments proportional to collected revenue and available capital, and build the organization one paid customer outcome at a time.
