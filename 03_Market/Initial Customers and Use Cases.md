# Initial Customers and Use Cases

## Purpose

Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) is being built to create paid technical work, hire qualified contributors, and deliver practical services for customers who need help with local information, maps, digital access, preservation, and web-connected tools.

This document identifies the first customer categories KTS intends to serve, the practical problems those customers face, the initial services KTS can offer, and the types of paid projects that can create a documented portfolio of customer outcomes.

KTS does not represent target customers, community contacts, demonstration opportunities, prospective collaborators, or organizations connected to its founder or contractors as current customers unless there is a written agreement, paid engagement, or completed authorized project.

## Market Entry Strategy

KTS will begin with practical, understandable services that solve an immediate problem for a customer. Each completed project can create revenue, support paid contractor work, produce a measurable result, and—with customer permission—become a portfolio example or case study.

```text
Secure a defined paid project
        ↓
Assign or hire the right delivery support
        ↓
Complete the agreed customer outcome
        ↓
Document the work, deliverables, and results
        ↓
Request approval for a testimonial or case study
        ↓
Use approved proof to pursue the next project
        ↓
Build recurring revenue and additional paid delivery capacity
```

KTS may begin with small fixed-price work and expand into larger mapping, preservation, managed-service, and ALLIS-node engagements only when customer needs, project scope, and available funding justify that growth.

## Initial Customer Categories

### Local Small Businesses

KTS will pursue mom-and-pop businesses and local service providers that need customers to find accurate information, understand where the business operates, get directions, contact the business, make a booking, place an order, or understand available services.

Potential customer types include:

- Restaurants, diners, bakeries, food trucks, caterers, and local food businesses.
- Retail stores, boutiques, antique shops, makers, crafters, and market vendors.
- Salons, barbers, nail technicians, tattoo businesses, and personal-service providers.
- Contractors, plumbers, electricians, cleaners, lawn-care providers, repair businesses, and other home-service businesses.
- Auto repair, detailing, towing, tire, and transportation businesses.
- Campgrounds, cabins, short-term rentals, venues, farms, wedding locations, and tourism businesses.
- Photographers, DJs, realtors, tutors, home-health providers, and mobile-service businesses.

Typical first KTS services may include:

- Get Found Locally setup or cleanup.
- Google Business Profile owner-support assistance.
- Find Us, directions, parking, pickup, or service-area page.
- QR-linked menu, services, booking, ordering, or information hub.
- Delivery-zone, service-area, venue, event, or customer-access map.
- Ongoing public-information and local-listing care plan.

### Website, Marketing, and Technology Firms

KTS will pursue collaboration with website firms, marketing agencies, designers, developers, IT providers, consultants, and technology companies that need mapping, location intelligence, data preparation, heritage content, or ALLIS-related support for their own clients.

KTS can serve as a subcontractor, specialist collaborator, white-label contributor, co-branded implementation partner, or referral partner when the project agreement permits it.

Typical first KTS services may include:

- Interactive maps and website map embedding.
- Service-area, delivery-zone, route, location, and point-of-interest maps.
- GIS data cleanup, address cleanup, geocoding, format conversion, and map-ready data preparation.
- QR-linked map, visitor, event, heritage, trail, or public-information tools.
- Local information architecture and approved content organization.
- Digital-heritage map features and archive-access tools.
- ALLIS discovery, pilot, implementation, or managed-service support.

### Heritage, Library, Archive, and Cultural Organizations

KTS will pursue projects with libraries, archives, museums, historical societies, churches, cemetery groups, universities, schools, local-history organizations, and cultural-resource partners that need maps, place-based records, photographs, documents, and community knowledge organized for preservation or access.

Typical first KTS services may include:

- Collection discovery and basic inventory.
- Digital Heritage Map Preservation assessment.
- Map, photograph, record, and document organization.
- Metadata, location indexing, map linking, and export preparation.
- Small public-history or heritage-map pilot.
- QR-linked historic-site, cemetery, trail, exhibit, or local-history information.
- Public web, kiosk, local Wi-Fi, researcher, staff, or offline-capable access planning.
- ALLIS archive-to-access pilot.

### Families, Genealogy, Cemetery, and Church Projects

KTS will pursue family-history, genealogy, cemetery, church-record, reunion, and local-history projects where customers need to preserve materials, organize records, connect information to places, and share approved content with family members, researchers, or the public.

Typical first KTS services may include:

- Family-archive discovery and inventory.
- Organization of approved family papers, photographs, records, maps, and oral-history materials.
- Cemetery, burial, church, family, migration, home-place, or reunion map.
- Private family archive, approved shared collection, or QR-linked family-history page.
- File naming, metadata, source organization, and documented preservation package.
- Private, family-controlled, public, kiosk, local-network, or ALLIS-based access where appropriate.

KTS does not guarantee proof of lineage, inheritance, citizenship, tribal enrollment, legal identity, or any genealogical conclusion. The customer or designated family steward remains responsible for relationship claims, source accuracy, privacy decisions, permissions, and public-release approval.

### Nonprofits, Community Organizations, and Grant Applicants

KTS will pursue organizations that need clear location-based information for a grant application, program, public resource, community event, outreach effort, tourism project, heritage initiative, or local service directory.

Typical first KTS services may include:

- Grant-ready project-area, service-area, resource, asset, route, or opportunity map.
- Community asset and resource inventory.
- Public service directory, points-of-interest map, or local-information page.
- Event, vendor, venue, trail, parking, visitor, or accessibility map.
- QR-linked outreach and public-information tool.
- Managed map updates, reporting dashboard, or project-location documentation.
- Preservation, public-access, or ALLIS-node discovery for a larger community-benefit project.

KTS does not guarantee grant awards, sponsor commitments, government approval, or public funding decisions.

### Municipal, Regional, Tourism, and Place-Based Organizations

KTS may pursue scoped work with municipalities, counties, regional-development organizations, tourism groups, downtown and Main Street organizations, trail groups, visitor organizations, and similar entities.

Potential first services may include:

- Public information, visitor, trail, event, parking, service, or resource maps.
- Community asset inventory and map organization.
- Grant-ready mapping and project-area visualization.
- Heritage, trail, downtown, cemetery, cultural-resource, and visitor-information pilots.
- Map and data cleanup, conversion, organization, and public-access preparation.
- Kiosk, local Wi-Fi, offline-capable, or ALLIS-node discovery and pilot work.

Projects involving regulated data, emergency response, public safety, utility operations, confidential records, or sensitive cultural resources require separate review of legal, security, governance, access, and procurement requirements before KTS accepts the work.

## Building the First Customer Stories

KTS does not need a large portfolio before beginning work. The company will create customer stories through paid, defined projects that deliver an observable result.

An initial customer story may be modest:

- A local business has corrected hours, phone number, address, directions, services, and map location.
- A customer receives a service-area map and a clear way for prospective clients to contact them.
- A restaurant, venue, event, trail, or shop receives a QR-linked information page or visitor map.
- A website firm receives an interactive map or location-data package for its client.
- A family, church, or cemetery group receives an organized map and preservation package.
- A heritage group receives its first inventory, digitized map set, map-based archive, or public-history pilot.
- A nonprofit receives an accurate map for a proposal, report, public directory, or community project.

When permitted by the customer, KTS will collect and retain:

- The approved project scope and deliverables.
- A proposal, agreement, invoice, payment record, or completed-project record.
- Before-and-after screenshots, maps, files, or other work samples.
- Customer approval to display project materials.
- Customer feedback, testimonial, or outcome statement.
- A clearly documented permission level: private, proposal-only, portfolio, or public case study.

## Customer Status and Public Claims

KTS uses the following categories to keep customer information accurate:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Current customer | A written agreement, paid engagement, or completed authorized project exists |
| Active prospect | A real project-specific conversation, referral, or proposal process is underway |
| Demonstration opportunity | A possible portfolio or pilot project with no current customer commitment |
| Referral or collaboration contact | A person or organization that may refer work or collaborate, without a current project commitment |
| Target customer segment | A customer category KTS intends to pursue; no individual customer relationship is implied |
| Community-benefit opportunity | A potential public-interest project that still requires funding, authorization, and written terms |

KTS will not publish a customer name, logo, testimonial, project image, data, map, or case study without the appropriate permission.

## First-Phase Customer Acquisition

KTS will begin customer development through practical, local, relationship-based outreach:

- Offer a defined Get Found Locally package to businesses with visible public-information problems.
- Prepare simple before-and-after examples of local listing, QR, directions, and service-area improvements.
- Contact website, marketing, design, and IT firms with a concise mapping and local-information capability sheet.
- Offer scoped grant-ready map packages to nonprofits, community groups, and project applicants.
- Identify heritage, genealogy, cemetery, church, library, and local-history groups with materials that would benefit from organization or map-based access.
- Build a standard intake form, proposal template, statement of work, contractor agreement, and customer-approval process.
- Maintain a private lead, proposal, project, payment, and follow-up record.
- Request portfolio and case-study permission only after a customer receives the agreed result.

## Initial Success Measures

During the first operating phase, KTS will measure progress through practical indicators:

- Number of qualified leads and customer conversations.
- Number and value of proposals issued.
- Number and value of paid projects secured.
- Number of contractors or contributors paid through project work.
- Number of completed deliverables and customer approvals.
- Number of approved portfolio examples, testimonials, or case studies.
- Amount of recurring monthly or annual service revenue.
- Number of partner-firm referrals or subcontracted projects.
- Customer retention, repeat work, and referral activity.

The first goal is not to claim scale before it exists. The goal is to complete useful paid work, document it accurately, build repeatable service packages, pay qualified contributors, and grow KTS through real customer outcomes.
