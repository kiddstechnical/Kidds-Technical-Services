# Partnership Pathways

## Purpose

Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) builds practical partnerships that expand local delivery capacity without misrepresenting ownership, authority, customer commitments, funding, or organizational relationships.

A partnership may involve referral, subcontracting, direct vendor services, co-branded implementation, shared project delivery, community-benefit stewardship, research collaboration, or participation in an approved MountainShares or ALLIS project. Each relationship is structured according to the actual work, customer authority, funding source, governance requirements, and written agreement.

KTS treats a partnership as a documented working relationship, not merely a conversation, shared interest, referral, community connection, use of the same technology, or interest in future collaboration.

## Partnership Principles

KTS uses the following principles in partnership development:

- Start with a practical customer, project, or community need.
- Define the actual role of each organization before representing a relationship publicly.
- Use the simplest lawful structure that fits the work.
- Keep customer authority, funding, contracts, invoices, accounts, and records clear.
- Match specialized skills to the project rather than claiming that one organization performs every function.
- Protect customer information, collection stewardship, privacy, public-release decisions, and intellectual-property rights.
- Document payment, ownership, support, maintenance, handoff, and exit responsibilities.
- Disclose and manage related-party relationships and conflicts of interest.
- Do not promise exclusivity, shared revenue, funding, ownership, customer work, or a future contract unless it is documented and authorized.

## Partnership Models

### Referral Partnership

A referral partnership exists when one organization identifies a potential customer or project and introduces that person or organization to KTS.

A referral does not create a contract, payment obligation, exclusive relationship, project commitment, ownership interest, or authority to speak for KTS.

A referral partner may include a website firm, marketing agency, chamber, tourism group, community organization, preservation consultant, library, nonprofit, contractor, local business, or individual.

KTS may independently evaluate the opportunity, scope the work, determine whether it is appropriate, and enter a direct agreement with the customer if the customer chooses to proceed.

```text
Partner identifies a potential customer need
        ↓
Partner introduces customer or project contact to KTS
        ↓
KTS and customer discuss needs independently
        ↓
KTS provides scope and proposal if appropriate
        ↓
Customer decides whether to engage KTS
```

Any referral fee, reciprocal referral arrangement, public endorsement, use of names or logos, or payment arrangement must be separately documented and lawful.

### Subcontractor Partnership

A subcontractor partnership exists when another firm or organization contracts with the customer and engages KTS to deliver a defined specialized component of the customer project.

KTS may serve as a subcontractor for mapping, GIS data preparation, address cleanup, geocoding, interactive map development, heritage-map work, digital preservation, QR-linked information tools, kiosk experiences, local-network access, offline-capable delivery, ALLIS support, training, documentation, or managed technical services.

The prime contractor remains responsible for the customer relationship unless the agreement states otherwise. KTS is responsible for its own agreed scope and must coordinate with the prime contractor on schedule, quality, confidentiality, access, approvals, billing, branding, and final delivery.

```text
Prime contractor identifies a customer need requiring KTS expertise
        ↓
Prime contractor and KTS define subcontract scope, price, schedule, and responsibilities
        ↓
Prime contractor contracts with customer
        ↓
KTS completes agreed specialist work
        ↓
Prime contractor pays KTS according to subcontract terms
```

### Direct Vendor Partnership

A direct vendor partnership exists when KTS contracts directly with the customer while coordinating with another organization involved in the same project.

This model is appropriate when the customer wants separate agreements, when KTS has a distinct technical scope, when the partner refers the customer but does not manage KTS’s work, or when each provider has different responsibilities.

```text
Customer identifies a project involving multiple providers
        ↓
Customer contracts directly with each provider for distinct scopes
        ↓
KTS coordinates with partner under customer-approved roles and access rules
        ↓
Each provider invoices customer separately unless another written arrangement applies
```

### White-Label or Co-Branded Partnership

KTS may provide work that is delivered under a partner firm’s brand, a shared customer brand, or a co-branded project identity when the parties agree in writing.

A white-label or co-branded arrangement must define:

- Whether KTS may be publicly named.
- Who communicates with the end customer.
- Which party owns or licenses the deliverables.
- How KTS’s work may be shown in a portfolio.
- Whether KTS may use its own branding, platform names, or ALLIS references.
- How support, updates, customer questions, and warranty requests are handled.
- Whether the arrangement is project-specific or continuing.

KTS will not represent white-label work as a direct KTS customer engagement without the partner’s and customer’s appropriate permission.

### Co-Delivery Partnership

A co-delivery partnership exists when KTS and another organization each contribute a defined role to the same customer or community project.

Examples include:

- A website firm designs and develops a website while KTS provides mapping and location-data services.
- A preservation consultant provides historical research while KTS organizes maps, metadata, public access, and technical delivery.
- A tourism organization provides approved visitor content while KTS creates map, QR, kiosk, or local-access tools.
- A photographer, videographer, or oral-history producer provides media while KTS organizes location-linked access.
- A planning, architecture, engineering, or consulting firm provides professional services while KTS provides non-engineering mapping, data preparation, and public-information tools.
- A community organization convenes participants while KTS provides technical implementation.

Each party remains responsible for its own professional scope, representations, licenses, insurance, legal obligations, and customer deliverables unless a written agreement provides otherwise.

### Research, Education, and Community Collaboration

KTS may collaborate with schools, universities, libraries, archives, museums, researchers, educators, students, community organizations, and cultural groups on approved research, learning, heritage, public-information, or community-access projects.

Potential collaboration activities include:

- Map-based research and local-information projects.
- Oral-history, archive, heritage, trail, cemetery, and family-history access projects.
- Student or volunteer-supported data organization under appropriate supervision.
- Community asset inventories and public-information mapping.
- Classroom, exhibit, library, visitor-center, kiosk, or local-network learning tools.
- Approved ALLIS or MountainShares-related research, learning, and public-benefit projects.

Research, education, and volunteer participation do not automatically create KTS ownership of materials, an employment relationship, a paid contract, a customer relationship, a public-release right, or authority to use personal, restricted, or sensitive information.

## Nonprofit Partnership Pathways

Nonprofits may choose the structure that fits their mission, project authority, funding, timeline, governance needs, and relationship with KTS.

### Direct Nonprofit-to-KTS Partnership

A nonprofit may engage KTS directly for a defined professional service. The nonprofit remains the customer and project steward, manages its own funding and approvals, and contracts with KTS as an independent technical-service provider.

This route is appropriate when the nonprofit has its own authorized project, available budget, decision-making authority, and need for a direct professional-services relationship.

KTS may provide mapping, public-information tools, archive organization, digital preservation, QR-linked experiences, website-connected map features, kiosk or local-access tools, ALLIS implementation, training, hosting coordination, and managed support.

The nonprofit remains responsible for confirming its funding authority, allowable costs, board approval, procurement requirements, grant restrictions, donor restrictions, privacy obligations, and public-release decisions.

### Harmony for Hope Community-Benefit Partnership

Harmony for Hope, Inc. (H4H) provides a potential pathway for qualifying community-benefit projects that need charitable stewardship, community coordination, fundraising, partner engagement, sponsor development, grant administration, or nonprofit-led project governance.

When a project aligns with H4H’s charitable mission and H4H accepts the project, H4H retains responsibility for charitable purpose, project governance, fund stewardship, donor and grant restrictions, beneficiary focus, public-benefit outcome, and applicable approval processes.

KTS may provide contracted technical implementation for an approved H4H project.

```text
Community need or project idea
        ↓
H4H mission and feasibility review
        ↓
H4H project approval, stewardship, and funding strategy
        ↓
Authorized grant, donor, sponsor, or project funds
        ↓
Written H4H–KTS scope, budget, deliverables, and approval process
        ↓
KTS technical implementation and project delivery
        ↓
Approved public-benefit access, handoff, support, or managed operation
```

H4H is not required to sponsor every KTS project. KTS is not automatically selected for H4H work. Each project requires independent mission review, authorized decision-making, documented funding, reasonable terms, conflict-of-interest safeguards, and a written agreement.

### MountainShares and ALLIS Collaboration

Some projects may be suited to a shared, community-governed, or commons-oriented MountainShares or ALLIS pathway rather than a conventional direct service contract or H4H-sponsored project.

Participation, access, contribution recognition, governance, eligibility, benefits, funding, and service levels are governed by applicable program rules, project agreements, access terms, and phased implementation requirements.

KTS may provide technical implementation, mapping, preservation, access tools, support, or ALLIS-related services within an approved MountainShares or community-governed project. Each such project must identify the steward or customer, authorized funding source, technical scope, access rules, ownership, support expectations, data-export terms, and public-benefit purpose.

No organization or individual receives automatic free access, automatic KTS services, ownership rights, funding, or partnership status merely by expressing interest in H4H, MountainShares, ALLIS, or a community-benefit project.

## Partnership Lifecycle

KTS develops partnerships through a practical sequence:

1. **Initial conversation:** The parties identify a potential customer, project, service gap, community need, or shared capability.
2. **Role definition:** Each party identifies what it can provide, who has customer authority, and whether KTS is a referral partner, subcontractor, direct vendor, co-delivery partner, or technical contributor.
3. **Feasibility review:** The parties consider scope, skills, schedule, capacity, customer needs, funding, legal requirements, privacy, rights, and risks.
4. **Written structure:** The parties document the appropriate agreement, scope, proposal, memorandum, subcontract, referral terms, or project plan.
5. **Customer or project approval:** The authorized customer, steward, lead organization, funder, or governing body approves the work as required.
6. **Delivery:** Each party performs its documented role and maintains appropriate communication, records, confidentiality, approvals, and quality control.
7. **Handoff or managed support:** The parties complete delivery, customer acceptance, documentation, payment, support, maintenance, export, and any agreed public communication.
8. **Review:** The parties evaluate the completed work and determine whether a future project, referral, case study, or ongoing arrangement is appropriate.

## Required Written Terms

The appropriate written agreement should identify:

- The customer, project steward, lead organization, and authorized decision-maker.
- Each party’s role, deliverables, responsibilities, exclusions, and schedule.
- Scope, price, payment terms, billing party, milestone requirements, and third-party costs.
- Customer communication, approval authority, change-control, and acceptance process.
- Confidentiality, privacy, data access, security, and stewardship requirements.
- Ownership, licensing, source files, maps, metadata, customer content, code, ALLIS configuration, and export rights.
- Branding, use of names and logos, portfolio rights, case-study approval, and public communications.
- Hosting, support, maintenance, updates, handoff, exit, and data-export responsibilities.
- Insurance, accessibility, procurement, grant, regulatory, professional-license, and risk requirements when applicable.
- Contractor status, subcontracting authority, and responsibility for each party’s personnel.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosures and related-party safeguards when applicable.
- Dispute-resolution, termination, and survival terms appropriate to the agreement.

## Related-Party and Conflict Safeguards

KTS maintains separation between commercial business activity and separate nonprofit, community, family, contractor, or affiliated organizations.

A related organization or individual is not automatically a KTS customer, funder, sponsor, partner, employee, contractor, owner, or authorized representative.

When a proposed partnership involves Harmony for Hope, Carrie Kidd, Aubrey Dickerson, another KTS contributor, a family relationship, board role, volunteer role, financial interest, or other material relationship, the relationship must be disclosed before selection, negotiation, approval, payment, or public representation.

The relevant organization should use an independent and authorized process. An interested person should not make or vote on the final decision to select, negotiate with, supervise, or approve payment to KTS where a conflict exists.

Every related-party transaction should use a written scope, reasonable and documented pricing, defined deliverables, appropriate records, separate accounts, and clear payment authority.

For additional boundaries, see `01_Company/Related-Party Boundaries.md`.

## Public Communications

KTS and its partners will describe relationships accurately.

Unless a written agreement states otherwise:

- A referral partner is not a KTS employee, agent, owner, or exclusive representative.
- A subcontractor relationship does not create a joint venture, partnership ownership, or shared customer authority.
- A co-delivery partner is responsible only for its documented scope.
- H4H is a separate nonprofit and may support qualifying projects through an approved pathway.
- MountainShares and ALLIS participation is governed by applicable program and project terms.
- A customer, partner, sponsor, funder, or project may not be named publicly without appropriate permission.
- KTS will not imply endorsement, ownership, funding, exclusivity, government approval, or project commitment that has not been documented.

## Operating Principle

KTS asks a simple question before entering any partnership:

> What real customer or community need are we solving, who has authority to approve the work, what is each party responsible for, and how will the work be funded, delivered, and maintained?

The best partnership is not the broadest promise. It is a clear working relationship that gives the customer or community a useful result, pays qualified contributors for real work, respects organizational boundaries, and creates a foundation for responsible future collaboration.
