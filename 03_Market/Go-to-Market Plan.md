# Go-to-Market Plan

## Purpose

Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) will enter the market by solving practical local-information problems that customers can understand and purchase immediately.

The initial goal is not to sell a complicated platform before customers understand the value. The goal is to secure defined paid work, deliver useful results, create documented proof, pay qualified contributors when needed, and build repeatable service packages that can grow into larger mapping, preservation, managed-service, partnership, and ALLIS-node opportunities.

KTS will use direct local outreach, partner-firm relationships, practical demonstrations, referrals, and approved project evidence to build demand.

## Market Entry Principle

KTS begins with this question:

> What would make it easier for this customer, visitor, staff member, family, or community to find, understand, preserve, or use information today?

The answer may be simple:

- Correct business hours and directions.
- A map showing a service area, event, trail, venue, parking location, or community resource.
- A QR page for a menu, booking link, services, historic site, or family collection.
- A grant-ready map.
- Organization of maps, photographs, records, genealogy materials, or local-history files.
- An interactive map or location-data feature for a website firm’s client.

KTS will start with the smallest useful paid solution and expand only when the customer’s needs justify a larger engagement.

## Initial Market Segments

### Local Small Businesses

KTS will focus first on mom-and-pop businesses that need better customer access, clearer public information, location visibility, directions, service-area information, QR tools, and simple map-connected web support.

Priority prospects include:

- Restaurants, diners, bakeries, caterers, food trucks, and local food businesses.
- Contractors, plumbers, electricians, cleaners, lawn-care providers, repair businesses, and mobile-service businesses.
- Retail stores, boutiques, makers, antique shops, craft businesses, and market vendors.
- Salons, barbers, personal-service businesses, photographers, DJs, and local professionals.
- Auto repair, towing, detailing, transportation, and tire businesses.
- Campgrounds, cabins, venues, farms, short-term rentals, event locations, and tourism businesses.

### Website, Marketing, Design, and Technology Firms

KTS will pursue website, marketing, branding, design, development, IT, planning, preservation, and consulting firms that need specialized location-based capabilities without hiring those skills in-house.

KTS will offer subcontracting, white-label support, direct vendor coordination, and co-delivery options for:

- Interactive maps and map embedding.
- GIS data cleanup, geocoding, address cleanup, and data preparation.
- Service-area, route, visitor, trail, event, and point-of-interest maps.
- Heritage, archive, genealogy, tourism, and public-information tools.
- QR-linked map and information experiences.
- ALLIS discovery, access tools, and implementation support.

### Heritage, Community, and Preservation Organizations

KTS will pursue libraries, archives, museums, historical societies, churches, cemetery groups, tourism organizations, trail groups, downtown organizations, schools, families, and community groups with place-based materials or public-information needs.

Initial services will emphasize practical first phases:

- Collection inventory and preservation assessment.
- Digital Heritage Map Preservation.
- Genealogy and family-history organization.
- Cemetery, church, heritage, trail, and visitor maps.
- Grant-ready maps and community asset inventories.
- QR-linked public-information pages.
- Small public map, kiosk, local-network, or ALLIS-access pilots.

## Sales Channels

### Direct Local Outreach

KTS will identify businesses and organizations with an observable customer-access, map, information, or preservation problem.

Examples include:

- Incorrect business hours, missing phone numbers, unclear directions, weak photos, outdated websites, or confusing map locations.
- Businesses with no clear service-area information.
- Venues, trails, events, restaurants, and shops without easy mobile directions, parking details, or QR access.
- Heritage groups, churches, cemeteries, and families with scattered maps, records, photographs, or place-based materials.
- Nonprofits and grant applicants that need a clear project-area, resource, or service map.

The direct outreach process will be:

```text
Identify a visible customer-access or information problem
        ↓
Complete a brief public-information review
        ↓
Contact the owner or authorized representative respectfully
        ↓
Explain one specific improvement KTS can deliver
        ↓
Offer a defined scope and price
        ↓
Follow up, document outcome, and request referral or portfolio permission after delivery
```

KTS will not make misleading claims, promise search-engine rankings, present speculative analysis as fact, use customer information without permission, or imply that a prospect has hired KTS.

### Partner-Firm Outreach

KTS will approach website, marketing, design, IT, preservation, planning, tourism, and consulting firms with a concise specialist capability statement.

The partnership message will be:

> KTS adds mapping, location intelligence, public-information tools, GIS data preparation, heritage-map features, QR experiences, and ALLIS-related support to projects where your client needs more than a standard website.

KTS will offer:

- A defined subcontractor rate or project quote.
- Clear communication and customer-approval boundaries.
- White-label, co-branded, or direct-vendor options.
- Confidentiality and appropriate data handling.
- A reliable handoff package for the partner and customer.
- Portfolio use only with approval.

### Community and Organization Outreach

KTS will reach out to appropriate heritage, tourism, library, archive, church, cemetery, family-history, nonprofit, trail, downtown, and local-community organizations.

The outreach focus will be on a practical first phase rather than a large proposal:

- “Would a collection inventory help you understand what you have?”
- “Would a grant-ready map make the project easier to explain?”
- “Would a QR-linked trail or visitor map help people use this resource?”
- “Would an organized preservation package help protect these records?”
- “Would a small map or archive pilot show whether a larger access project is worthwhile?”

Organizations may choose a direct KTS service agreement, an H4H community-benefit pathway when appropriate and accepted, or a MountainShares/ALLIS pathway under applicable program rules and agreements.

### Referral and Repeat Business

KTS will ask satisfied customers and partner firms for referrals after completing work and receiving customer acceptance.

A referral request should be simple:

> If you know another business, organization, family, or project that needs clearer maps, easier public information, better location access, or help organizing local records, we would appreciate an introduction.

KTS may offer referral arrangements only when they are documented, lawful, and appropriate to the relationship.

## Sales Process

KTS will use a consistent process for customer leads.

### 1. Lead Identification

Record the prospect, source, contact, observed need, segment, potential first offer, and next action in a private lead log.

### 2. Discovery Conversation

Confirm:

- The customer’s problem and desired outcome.
- The authorized decision-maker.
- Customer timeline and budget range.
- Current website, maps, records, source materials, or public-information condition.
- Required access, privacy, ownership, and approval boundaries.
- Whether the customer is paying directly, using a partner firm, seeking funding, or considering another pathway.

### 3. Scope and Proposal

Provide a practical written proposal that identifies:

- Customer need and intended outcome.
- Deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, timeline, and revisions.
- Price, deposit, milestone, or payment terms.
- Customer responsibilities and required source materials.
- Ownership, licensing, access, public-release, and export terms.
- Third-party costs and optional ongoing support.

### 4. Authorization and Payment

KTS begins work after the appropriate customer authorization, funding confirmation, deposit or payment arrangement, and required access or materials are in place.

### 5. Delivery and Acceptance

KTS completes the agreed work, obtains customer review, makes included revisions, and documents final acceptance.

### 6. Follow-Up and Expansion

After delivery, KTS may offer:

- A care plan or managed update service.
- A related map, QR, data, archive, or public-information package.
- A larger preservation or ALLIS discovery project.
- Referral support or approved case-study participation.

## Demonstration Projects

KTS may create internal demonstrations, test environments, sample maps, and fictional or clearly labeled illustrative examples to explain its services.

KTS will not present a demonstration as a customer project, public partnership, grant-funded project, community endorsement, or completed paid engagement unless it is documented and authorized.

Approved customer work may be used as a portfolio example only with permission from the customer, collection steward, partner firm, or other authorized party.

## First 90 Days

### Days 1–30: Prepare to Sell

- Finalize service documents, pricing ranges, customer pathways, partnership pathways, and standard boundaries.
- Prepare a one-page overview and simple service handouts.
- Establish a business email, phone process, intake form, proposal template, invoice process, and lead tracker.
- Create three to five sample visual examples that demonstrate small-business, heritage, map, QR, or public-information work.
- Identify an initial list of local businesses and partner firms with visible potential needs.
- Meet with WV SBDC or another qualified business advisor for review of pricing, cash flow, sales process, hiring, financing, and business planning.

### Days 31–60: Begin Outreach

- Contact at least 10 local businesses with a specific, respectful observation and defined entry offer.
- Contact at least 5 website, marketing, design, IT, preservation, planning, or consulting firms with a capability statement.
- Contact at least 5 heritage, community, tourism, library, church, cemetery, genealogy, or nonprofit organizations regarding practical first-phase needs.
- Hold discovery conversations and create proposals for qualified prospects.
- Track lead source, service interest, proposal value, next action, and status.
- Review which offers generate the most interest and revise outreach language as needed.

### Days 61–90: Deliver and Document

- Secure and complete the first defined paid projects.
- Engage qualified contractors or contributors when a project requires additional delivery capacity.
- Obtain customer acceptance and retain project documentation.
- Request approved testimonials, referrals, screenshots, and portfolio permission.
- Identify opportunities for care plans, repeat work, partner-firm collaboration, or larger follow-on services.
- Review pricing, time required, delivery costs, contractor cost, margin, customer feedback, and scope-control lessons.
- Set the next 90-day target based on actual sales data.

## Success Measures

KTS will measure early go-to-market progress through:

- Number of qualified leads identified.
- Number of discovery conversations completed.
- Number and value of proposals issued.
- Proposal acceptance rate.
- Number and value of paid projects.
- Average project value and gross margin.
- Number of paid contractor assignments supported by project revenue.
- Number of repeat customers and managed-service customers.
- Number of referrals from customers and partner firms.
- Number of approved testimonials, case studies, screenshots, or portfolio examples.
- Time from first contact to payment.
- Customer satisfaction, acceptance, and referral feedback.

KTS will adjust service packages, pricing, delivery process, partner outreach, and marketing assets based on real customer evidence rather than assumptions.

## Boundaries

KTS will not:

- Guarantee search ranking, grant awards, funding decisions, customer traffic, revenue, visitor counts, or other outcomes outside the agreed scope.
- Claim a customer, project, grant, sponsor, funder, partnership, or government relationship before it is documented and authorized.
- Treat H4H, MountainShares, ALLIS, a related organization, or a community connection as an automatic customer-acquisition channel or funding source.
- Use customer, partner, family, collection, project, or personal information without appropriate permission.
- Begin work without clear scope, customer authority, payment terms, and approval expectations.
- Accept work that requires credentials, licensing, security controls, legal review, procurement steps, or regulated-data practices that KTS is not qualified or authorized to provide.

## Operating Principle

KTS will grow through useful work that customers can understand, authorize, and pay for.

The first objective is not to appear large. It is to complete real projects, create real customer outcomes, pay qualified contributors for needed work, document evidence responsibly, and build a durable local business capable of delivering increasingly valuable mapping, preservation, information-access, partnership, and ALLIS services.
