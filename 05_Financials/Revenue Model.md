# Revenue Model

## Purpose

This document describes how Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) plans to earn revenue through defined professional services.

KTS earns revenue by scoping, pricing, contracting, delivering, invoicing, and supporting technical-service work for customers. KTS may provide mapping, local-information systems, web-connected services, digital-preservation work, archives and heritage services, data preparation, interactive information tools, managed technical support, training, and partner-firm subcontracting.

This document is a planning model. It is not a guarantee of customer demand, revenue, profitability, financing, grant approval, partnership approval, staffing level, contract award, or future market conditions.

KTS will revise pricing, service packages, volume assumptions, margins, payment terms, and revenue targets when actual sales, contract values, direct costs, customer feedback, competitor conditions, and operating records become available.

## Revenue Boundary

### ALLIS Boundary

ALLIS is not included in this revenue model.

KTS will not count prospective ALLIS revenue, product licensing, platform subscriptions, deployments, customer adoption, future commercialization, or platform value as current revenue, contracted backlog, recurring revenue, financing support, or justification for hiring.

KTS may separately document ALLIS research, readiness work, governance, internal development, and future commercialization planning. Those activities remain outside this document until ALLIS has a market-ready offering, authorized customer terms, defined pricing, support requirements, delivery costs, and a lawful contractual pathway.

## Revenue Principles

KTS will use the following revenue principles:

- Sell services that KTS can lawfully and responsibly deliver now.
- Use written scopes, statements of work, proposals, estimates, service agreements, invoices, and acceptance criteria.
- Price each project to cover direct delivery costs and contribute to KTS operating costs.
- Target at least 50% gross margin on standard fixed-price work after direct project costs.
- Use deposits, milestone billing, advance service payments, or documented funding authority before contractor-heavy work begins.
- Avoid treating unpaid interest, informal discussions, grant applications, potential sponsorships, or verbal customer interest as revenue.
- Separate pass-through customer costs from KTS service revenue when practical.
- Track revenue by service line, customer type, project, source of lead, collected cash, direct cost, gross margin, and payment status.
- Decline, defer, re-scope, or re-price work that does not support customer outcomes, KTS capacity, legal obligations, or financial sustainability.

## Revenue Architecture

```text
Lead, referral, partner relationship, inquiry, or procurement opportunity
        ↓
Discovery conversation and customer need
        ↓
Defined scope, price, delivery timeline, and acceptance criteria
        ↓
Written proposal, service agreement, statement of work, or purchase order
        ↓
Deposit, advance payment, milestone authorization, or confirmed funding pathway
        ↓
Authorized KTS delivery and contractor work
        ↓
Customer review, acceptance, invoice, and collection
        ↓
Case study, renewal, referral, managed service, training, or follow-on work
```

KTS does not treat a lead, conversation, letter of interest, draft proposal, grant application, or verbal agreement as revenue until the appropriate written and financial authorization exists.

## Pricing and Gross Margin

KTS should price projects using a documented delivery-cost model.

```text
Customer project price
        − direct contractor labor
        − dedicated software, hosting, equipment, travel, scanning,
          printing, research, data acquisition, and vendor costs
        = gross project margin
        − KTS operating-cost allocation
        = contribution toward compensation, reserve, debt service,
          reinvestment, and future delivery capacity
```

| Pricing Component | Required Consideration |
|---|---|
| Direct labor | Contractor or employee hours, rates, project management, quality review, revisions, and customer support |
| Direct project expense | Travel, equipment, scanning, printing, data acquisition, specialized software, hosting, vendors, and permits |
| Operating-cost contribution | Sales, administration, software, insurance, accounting, legal, communications, workspace, and management |
| Risk and contingency | Scope uncertainty, customer readiness, data quality, schedule risk, dependency risk, and delivery complexity |
| Margin | KTS targets at least 50% gross margin on standard fixed-price work after direct project costs |
| Payment terms | Deposits, milestones, acceptance, collections risk, and customer funding pathway |
| Strategic value | Portfolio value, referral potential, recurring-service opportunity, partner relationship, and mission fit |

Example planning calculation:

```text
Customer project price:               $10,000
Direct contractor cost:              − $3,000
Direct tools, travel, vendor cost:   − $1,000
------------------------------------------------
Gross project margin:                 $6,000
Gross margin:                           60%
```

KTS may use another gross-margin target for strategic pilots, pass-through work, grant-funded projects, subcontracting, competitive bids, or unusual customer value, but the project must be deliberately approved and must not create an unsupported cash-flow or delivery burden.

## Payment Terms

KTS will use payment terms designed to protect customer delivery, contractor payments, and operating cash flow.

| Project Size or Service Type | Planning Payment Structure |
|---|---|
| Under $1,000 | Full payment before delivery or at customer approval |
| $1,000–$5,000 | 50% deposit before work; 50% before launch or handoff |
| $5,000–$25,000 | 40% deposit; 40% at implementation milestone; 20% at customer acceptance |
| Over $25,000 | 25%–40% deposit; monthly or milestone billing; final balance at acceptance |
| Managed service | Monthly, quarterly, or annual payment in advance |
| Training | Full payment in advance, deposit and balance, or purchase order |
| Grant-funded, public, nonprofit, sponsor-funded, or partner-funded work | Written contract, purchase order, funding authority, or authorized agreement before contractor-heavy work begins |

KTS will not begin contractor-heavy, travel-heavy, equipment-heavy, or vendor-heavy work until the required deposit, milestone authorization, customer purchase order, funding authorization, or other documented payment pathway exists.

## Pipeline Stages

KTS should track each prospective opportunity using defined stages.

| Pipeline Stage | Meaning | Revenue Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | A possible customer, referral, inquiry, or opportunity exists | Not revenue |
| Qualified lead | KTS understands a plausible need, contact, budget range, and timeline | Not revenue |
| Discovery | KTS is gathering scope, data, goals, constraints, and customer requirements | Not revenue |
| Proposal submitted | KTS has provided a written scope and price | Not revenue |
| Verbal interest | Customer expresses intent but has not authorized work or payment | Not revenue |
| Contract or purchase order received | Written authorization exists | Track as contracted backlog, not collected revenue |
| Deposit or first payment collected | KTS has received payment | Collected revenue or deferred revenue, depending on accounting treatment |
| Active delivery | KTS is performing authorized work | Revenue recognition follows KTS accounting method and contract terms |
| Customer acceptance | Customer accepts defined deliverables | Final invoice or milestone collection may be due |
| Completed and collected | Customer work is complete and payment is received | Collected revenue |

KTS should use qualified accounting support to determine the appropriate accounting treatment for deposits, deferred revenue, invoices, revenue recognition, taxes, accounts receivable, contractor costs, and financial reporting.

## Revenue Controls

KTS will use the following controls before recognizing a project as financially viable:

- Confirm customer authority, budget, payment pathway, and decision-maker.
- Use written scope, deliverables, timeline, acceptance criteria, exclusions, ownership terms, confidentiality terms, and change-control rules.
- Estimate direct labor, contractor cost, travel, vendor cost, software, equipment, hosting, data acquisition, tax, and contingency before quoting.
- Obtain deposits, milestone payments, purchase orders, funding authority, or other documented payment protection before authorizing substantial delivery costs.
- Keep project budgets, contractor authorizations, invoices, time records, receipts, and customer approvals.
- Track direct costs and gross margin by project.
- Pause, re-scope, or seek written change authorization when work falls outside the approved scope.
- Avoid using one customer’s payment to conceal losses or unfunded delivery obligations on another customer project.
- Keep KTS funds separate from customer, donor, grant, nonprofit, contractor, and personal funds unless a written agreement authorizes a specific arrangement.

## Update Process

KTS will review the revenue model at least quarterly and after material changes in services, pricing, customer demand, staffing, cash flow, financing, or market conditions.

KTS should compare projections with:

- Leads, qualified opportunities, proposals, contracts, backlog, invoices, collections, and accounts receivable.
- Revenue by service line, customer segment, referral source, project, and recurring-service agreement.
- Direct project costs, contractor hours, vendor costs, gross margin, operating-cost allocation, and collection timing.
- Average project value, proposal conversion, sales cycle length, repeat work, customer retention, renewals, and referrals.
- Marketing expense, partner-development activity, customer-acquisition cost, and documented return on investment.
- Available contractor capacity, project schedule, quality-review capacity, and working capital.

## Operating Principle

> KTS earns revenue through real, authorized, and professionally delivered services. KTS prices work to protect customer outcomes, contributor pay, operating capacity, working capital, and long-term sustainability.

KTS grows through collected revenue, documented margins, repeatable service delivery, recurring customer relationships, disciplined payment terms, responsible use of contractor capacity, and transparent financial records.
