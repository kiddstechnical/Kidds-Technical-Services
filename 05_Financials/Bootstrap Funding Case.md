# Bootstrap Funding Case

> **Kidd’s Technical Services, LLC**  
> Built on trust. Grounded in what’s real.

## Purpose

This document explains a staged bootstrap funding request for Kidd’s Technical Services, LLC (KTS).

The purpose of funding is to help KTS establish the minimum operating capacity needed to sell, deliver, document, and repeat practical local-business services. Funding is not a claim that KTS already has large recurring revenue, immediate payroll capacity, a fully staffed team, or commercialized ALLIS revenue.

KTS will use existing capability first, seek customer deposits before material delivery work, and add costs only when a documented business need, customer project, collected revenue, or approved working-capital plan supports them.

This is a planning document. It is not an offer of securities, loan approval request, revenue guarantee, employment offer, contractor commitment, customer contract, or promise of funding.

## Funding Request Structure

KTS proposes two possible bootstrap funding levels:

| Funding stage | Request | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Stage 1 | $25,000 | Establish the minimum operating foundation, validate customer demand, complete early paid projects, and protect a controlled working-capital reserve |
| Stage 2 | Up to an additional $25,000 | Expand only after KTS demonstrates customer demand, reliable delivery, documented financial controls, and a justified use of additional capital |
| Maximum staged request | $50,000 | A potential two-stage capitalization pathway; not a request to spend $50,000 immediately |

KTS should not treat the Stage 2 amount as available unless it is separately approved, documented, received, and appropriate for the business’s demonstrated needs.

## Why a Staged Request

A staged structure is more disciplined than assuming that KTS needs a large upfront investment before it has proven the local-business offer.

Stage 1 allows KTS to:

- Establish basic operations, records, service materials, invoicing, bookkeeping, insurance review, and customer-delivery capability.
- Conduct direct outreach and customer discovery.
- Submit real proposals and collect deposits.
- Deliver small, fixed-price projects using existing tools where possible.
- Document actual customer demand, actual direct costs, actual delivery time, actual margins, and actual operating expenses.
- Avoid premature commitments to payroll, subscriptions, office space, equipment, contractors, advertising, or product development.
- Preserve working capital for real timing gaps, project-specific delivery needs, and customer-funded work.

Stage 2 is considered only if KTS can show that additional capital would address a real and documented constraint, such as a credible paid-project pipeline, customer-payment timing, essential delivery equipment, project-funded contractor support, insurance requirements, or a defined revenue-producing expansion.

## Bootstrap Use-of-Funds Principles

KTS will use funding only for documented, business-related, and authorized purposes.

Funding priorities are:

1. Minimum legal, financial, insurance, bookkeeping, invoicing, recordkeeping, and operating readiness.
2. Essential tools, equipment, storage, security, and communications that KTS cannot deliver approved paid work without.
3. Direct local-business outreach, customer discovery, proposal preparation, and customer-acquisition activity.
4. Project-specific costs required to deliver signed customer work.
5. Controlled working capital to manage reasonable startup and customer-payment timing gaps.
6. Flexible staffing support only when a defined customer project, documented delivery need, and available budget support it.

KTS will not use bootstrap funding for:

- Personal living expenses, personal debt, family expenses, or caregiver expenses.
- Founder payroll during the bootstrap stage.
- Standing employee payroll, large recurring contractor commitments, or compensation promises without sustained collections and documented available cash.
- Unapproved equipment, speculative technology purchases, or unnecessary software subscriptions.
- A new website build when the existing KTS web presence can support the immediate launch.
- ALLIS product commercialization, subscription revenue assumptions, deployments, payroll, staffing, debt repayment, or projected revenue.
- Harmony for Hope, MountainShares, donor, grant, customer, contractor, personal, or other-entity expenses unless a written agreement specifically authorizes an identified KTS transaction.
- Untracked, undocumented, non-business, prohibited, or unauthorized spending.

## Working-Capital and Flexible-Staffing Reserve

The $10,000 Stage 1 working-capital and flexible-staffing reserve is not an immediate payroll pool and is not a promise of employment or contractor work.

It exists to protect KTS from avoidable early-stage cash-flow pressure while it builds a repeatable cycle of outreach, proposals, customer deposits, delivery, final payment, and follow-on work.

The reserve may be used only for documented and authorized needs, such as:

- A short timing gap between an approved project expense and customer payment.
- A direct delivery cost that is necessary to complete signed customer work.
- A limited contractor engagement with a written scope, rate, confidentiality terms, classification review, invoice process, and not-to-exceed amount.
- Essential temporary support needed to meet a paid project deadline or customer requirement.
- A validated operating continuity need that protects existing customer obligations.
- A business expense that cannot reasonably be funded from a customer deposit but is necessary for KTS to deliver approved paid work.

The reserve will not be used for:

- Founder salary or personal expenses.
- Guaranteed weekly contractor hours.
- Open-ended contractor arrangements.
- Employee payroll before payroll readiness, employment compliance review, sustained collections, and available cash.
- Work without a written customer scope or documented operational need.
- ALLIS activities, product development, commercialization, assumed subscription revenue, debt repayment, or payroll.
- Expenses belonging to another organization or funding source.

## Stage 2: Up to $25,000

Stage 2 should be considered only after KTS documents that it has progressed beyond planning and has a credible reason to deploy more capital.

Potential Stage 2 evidence may include:

- Paid customers and completed customer projects.
- Customer deposits and final payments collected.
- A documented pipeline of qualified prospects, written proposals, signed scopes, or recurring-service opportunities.
- Actual project margins and direct delivery costs.
- Reliable bookkeeping, invoice, payment, expense, and customer-approval records.
- Defined equipment, software, insurance, storage, security, or staffing constraints that are limiting paid delivery.
- A cash-flow analysis showing that additional capital would enable revenue-producing work rather than speculative spending.
- A plan for repayment, lender reporting, grant reporting, or other accountability obligations, if applicable.

If approved, Stage 2 funding may support:

| Potential use | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Additional essential equipment | Must be tied to a documented delivery constraint, approved budget, and expected business benefit |
| Increased customer-acquisition capacity | Must use a measurable outreach or partnership plan with tracked costs and results |
| Project-funded contractors | Must be tied to paid work, a written scope, available cash, and appropriate worker-classification review |
| Expanded storage, security, backup, or collaboration tools | Must be driven by real customer, data, capacity, or continuity needs |
| Insurance or compliance requirements | Must be based on actual quotes, customer requirements, or a new documented risk |
| Limited operating reserve | Must be justified by actual receivables, cash-flow timing, and planned paid delivery |

## Revenue and Payroll Boundaries

KTS does not assume immediate large revenue, immediate founder payroll, employee payroll, or a full-time contractor team.

The bootstrap plan is built around small, fixed-price, customer-funded local-business services, including:

- Verified local business information.
- Trusted information hubs.
- Customer-access pages and QR-linked information.
- Local access maps.
- Location, parking, pickup, entrance, service-area, event, and directions information.
- Approved update, care, or maintenance work when KTS defines a sustainable offering.

KTS may set a future founder-compensation goal, such as $100,000 annually, but that is not current payroll and is not an authorized use of Stage 1 funds. Founder compensation can be considered only after sustained collections, cash-flow review, taxes, obligations, reserves, and available funds support it.

KTS will not hire employees or make standing contractor commitments solely because funding is received. Staffing decisions require:

- A documented business need.
- A defined role and measurable deliverables.
- A written arrangement.
- Appropriate legal, tax, insurance, and classification review.
- Available cash after current obligations, required reserves, and project costs.
- A reasonable expectation that the role can be sustained or is appropriately limited to a funded project.

## ALLIS Boundary

ALLIS is not a source of current KTS revenue in this funding case.

This document does not assume:

- ALLIS product sales.
- Subscription revenue.
- Customer contracts.
- Product commercialization.
- Deployment revenue.
- Licensing revenue.
- Investor proceeds.
- ALLIS-funded payroll.
- ALLIS debt repayment.
- A future technical product outcome as the basis for KTS’s immediate operating budget.

KTS may separately explore future technology, research, or product opportunities only through distinct planning, funding, ownership, security, governance, legal, and financial processes. Those activities must not be represented as current KTS revenue or used to justify bootstrap spending without written authorization.

## Financial Controls

KTS will maintain clear records for all funding received and spent.

At a minimum, KTS will:

- Use a dedicated KTS business account for KTS funds.
- Record the source, date, terms, restrictions, and authorization for each funding transaction.
- Retain invoices, receipts, contracts, quotes, approvals, and supporting records.
- Track spending by the use-of-funds categories in this document.
- Record customer deposits, final payments, refunds, direct project costs, contractor costs, and operating expenses separately.
- Review cash on hand, accounts receivable, unpaid obligations, project commitments, and reserve balance at least monthly.
- Obtain any required lender, funder, investor, donor, grantor, board, or partner approval before reallocating restricted funds.
- Maintain separate records for KTS, personal activity, Harmony for Hope, MountainShares, ALLIS, and any other entity or project.
- Avoid making claims about revenues, funding, customers, product capability, insurance, staffing, or future results that KTS cannot document.

## Stage-Gate Review

Before moving from Stage 1 to Stage 2, KTS should prepare a short review containing:

1. Funding received, restrictions, and use-to-date.
2. Customer outreach activity and customer-discovery results.
3. Proposals issued, deposits collected, projects completed, and final payments collected.
4. Revenue, direct project costs, operating costs, cash on hand, accounts receivable, and remaining reserve.
5. Actual delivery capacity and any customer-service, equipment, storage, insurance, security, or staffing constraints.
6. Evidence supporting the need for additional funding.
7. A revised use-of-funds plan based on actual—not assumed—costs and demand.
8. Any required reporting, repayment, compliance, or approval obligations.
9. A recommendation to proceed, pause, revise, or decline Stage 2.

## Success Standard

The funding case succeeds when KTS can demonstrate that it has used capital responsibly to build a disciplined customer-revenue cycle:

```text
Local outreach
    ↓
Customer discovery
    ↓
Written scope and proposal
    ↓
Customer deposit
    ↓
Documented delivery
    ↓
Customer approval and final payment
    ↓
Accurate records, case evidence, referral, care plan, or follow-on work
    ↓
Repeat with measured costs and stronger operating capacity
```

The goal is not to spend capital quickly. The goal is to use limited capital carefully, prove customer demand, protect trust, and create a sustainable path from early paid work to reliable operations.
