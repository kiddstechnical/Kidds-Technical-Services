# Financial Projections

## Purpose

This document provides scenario-based financial projections for Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS).

The projections help KTS plan a sustainable technical-services organization that can pay qualified contributors, cover ordinary operating costs, protect working capital, and deliver mapping, local-information, digital-preservation, web-connected, and managed technical services.

These figures are planning assumptions. They are not historical financial statements, tax returns, audited financials, a valuation, loan approval, grant approval, legal advice, accounting advice, insurance advice, or a guarantee of future revenue.

KTS will update this document when it has actual customer contracts, deposits, invoices, collections, contractor agreements, payroll costs, insurance quotes, software costs, equipment costs, financing terms, and professional-service quotes.

## Projection Boundary

### ALLIS Boundary

ALLIS is not included in projected KTS revenue, contracted backlog, recurring revenue, financing collateral, staffing justification, or operating assumptions at this stage.

KTS may separately document ALLIS research, readiness work, governance, internal development, and future commercialization planning. Those activities do not support this forecast until ALLIS has an authorized market offering, defined pricing, delivery costs, customer terms, support requirements, and a lawful contractual pathway.

KTS must not rely on possible future ALLIS revenue to justify current payroll, contractor commitments, business financing, or debt-repayment assumptions.

## Payment Terms and Cash-Flow Protection

KTS uses payment terms designed to protect contractor payments, operating capacity, and working capital.

| Project Size or Service Type | Planning Payment Structure |
|---|---|
| Under $1,000 | Full payment before delivery or at customer approval |
| $1,000–$5,000 | 50% deposit before work; 50% before launch or handoff |
| $5,000–$25,000 | 40% deposit; 40% at implementation milestone; 20% at customer acceptance |
| Over $25,000 | 25%–40% deposit; monthly or milestone billing; final balance at acceptance |
| Managed service | Monthly, quarterly, or annual payment in advance |
| Grant-funded, public, nonprofit, sponsor-funded, or partner-funded work | Written funding authority, contract, purchase order, or authorized agreement before KTS staffs contractor-heavy work |

KTS does not begin contractor-heavy work until the customer pays the required deposit or an authorized project pathway confirms funding and the payment schedule.

## Project Economics

KTS prices projects to cover production work and organizational capacity.

```text
Project price
        − direct contractor labor
        − dedicated software, hosting, equipment, travel, printing, scanning, and vendor costs
        = gross project margin
        − KTS operating-cost allocation
        = contribution toward compensation, reserves, debt service, and growth
```

Example planning calculation:

```text
Project price:                         $10,000
Direct contractor cost:               − $3,000
Direct tools, travel, and vendor cost − $1,000
------------------------------------------------
Gross project margin:                  $6,000
Gross margin:                            60%
```

KTS targets at least 50% gross margin on standard fixed-price work after direct project costs. KTS may use another margin target for strategic pilots, partner work, pass-through costs, competitive bids, grant-funded work, or projects with unusual customer value, provided that it documents the reason and protects cash flow.

## Staffing Triggers

KTS scales staffing based on funded work, collections, gross margins, recurring revenue, contractor availability, and working capital.

| Business Condition | Staffing Action |
|---|---|
| Individual small projects | Carrie leads delivery; KTS uses contractors only when the project budget supports them |
| Repeated paid projects | KTS establishes regular project coordination, GIS, web, preservation, or support contractor capacity |
| Recurring managed-service revenue | KTS retains support, systems, mapping, and customer-success capacity |
| Sustained profitable workload | KTS considers part-time or full-time employee roles after payroll, benefits, supervision, insurance, and cash-flow review |
| Large funded project | KTS assigns defined contractor or employee roles within the approved project budget |
| Working-capital funding secured | KTS may reserve qualified contractor capacity, build operating systems, and prepare for planned hiring |

KTS will not treat a funding goal or projected revenue amount as permission to create unfunded payroll, contractor, or vendor obligations.

## Key Risks and Controls

| Risk | KTS Planning Response |
|---|---|
| Customer payment delays | Use deposits, milestone billing, clear invoices, documented acceptance, and working-capital reserve |
| Contractor costs exceed project budget | Use project-specific scopes, not-to-exceed authorizations, time tracking, and change approval |
| Revenue depends too heavily on small projects | Build partner-firm, institutional, heritage, mapping, managed-service, and training revenue streams |
| Revenue depends too heavily on one customer or partner | Diversify customer types, service types, and funding pathways |
| Large project requires costly work before payment | Confirm funding authority, deposits, milestones, and contractor budget before staffing |
| Insurance, payroll, HR, accounting, legal, or marketing costs exceed estimates | Obtain quotes, update projections, and maintain contingency reserve |
| Contractor classification risk | Use qualified HR, payroll, accounting, and legal review before establishing long-term working arrangements |
| Customer scope expands without payment | Use written change control and separate authorization for additional work |
| Managed-service obligation exceeds revenue | Define service limits, update frequency, response expectations, and renewal terms in writing |
| Financing adds repayment pressure | Match financing amount and repayment terms to documented revenue, cash flow, and working-capital need |
| Unready product or platform revenue is included in forecasts | Keep ALLIS and other unmarket-ready products outside revenue and financing projections |

## Update Process

KTS will update this document using actual business data.

At least quarterly, KTS should compare:

- Projected revenue with invoiced revenue, collected revenue, and contracted backlog.
- Projected contractor capacity with authorized, billed, and paid contractor hours.
- Project prices with direct project costs and gross margin.
- Payroll, HR, accounting, insurance, software, hosting, marketing, and equipment assumptions with actual costs.
- Deposits, milestone billing, collections, accounts receivable, and cash on hand.
- Managed-service revenue with actual support, hosting, update, and staffing costs.
- Working-capital needs with actual payment timing and project-delivery obligations.
- Staffing plans with customer demand, available budget, and profitability.

KTS will revise forecasts when actual financial records show that a planning assumption is inaccurate.

## Operating Principle

KTS uses financial projections to build a durable organization, not to create unsupported claims.

> KTS earns revenue by delivering defined professional services and managed technical support. KTS uses that revenue and appropriate capital to pay qualified contributors, cover delivery and operating costs, maintain working capital, and build a reliable technical-services organization.

The Moonshot Organization Scenario provides a high-capacity target. The Foundational and Growth Scenarios provide a practical path toward that target through paid customer work, documented margins, disciplined billing, recurring revenue, careful marketing, and responsible staffing decisions.
