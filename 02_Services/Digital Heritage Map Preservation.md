# Digital Heritage Map Preservation

## Purpose

Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) helps organizations, families, and communities preserve and make responsible use of maps, place-based records, photographs, documents, and related local-history materials.

The service is designed for collections that are valuable but difficult to use because they are scattered, fragile, paper-based, stored in old formats, poorly organized, difficult to search, or not connected to the places they describe.

KTS helps collection stewards organize approved materials into a practical preservation and access system. Depending on the project, the result may be an organized digital collection, a map-based archive, a public web experience, a staff or researcher tool, a kiosk, a local Wi-Fi information point, an offline-capable collection, or a documented preservation package.

Digital preservation is not only scanning. A usable preservation project also needs organization, descriptive information, quality checks, clear rights decisions, copies, documentation, and a plan for future access. National archival guidance similarly emphasizes selection, preparation, metadata, quality control, access review, digital conversion, and sustainable storage as connected parts of digitization and preservation work. [160][244]

## Materials KTS Can Help Organize

Projects may include customer-approved materials such as:

- Historic and contemporary maps.
- Plat maps, fire-insurance maps, land-use maps, transportation maps, trail maps, and community maps.
- Cemetery, church, school, neighborhood, family, business, or industrial-history records.
- Historic photographs, postcards, brochures, newspaper clippings, reports, oral-history materials, and research files.
- Geographic-information-system files, spreadsheets, address lists, location records, and field documentation.
- Architectural, cultural-landscape, archaeological, or historic-resource documentation where the customer has the right to use and share it.
- Visitor, trail, downtown, tourism, community-resource, and place-based public-information materials.

Cultural-resource work commonly relies on both geographic information and descriptive documentation. The National Park Service’s cultural-resources GIS work uses mapping, metadata, and data-management standards to support cultural-resource stewardship. [243][245]

## What KTS Provides

### Collection Discovery and Planning

KTS begins by helping the collection steward identify what exists, what matters most, what can be shared, and what a practical first phase should accomplish.

A discovery phase may include:

- Basic collection inventory.
- Identification of priority maps, records, images, or locations.
- Review of file condition, formats, organization, and apparent duplication.
- Identification of intended users, such as staff, researchers, visitors, students, customers, or the public.
- Review of known rights, restrictions, privacy concerns, cultural sensitivity, and approval responsibilities.
- Recommendation for a practical preservation, access, and delivery approach.

### Digitization Coordination and Preparation

KTS may prepare customer materials for digitization, coordinate an agreed digitization workflow, or organize customer-supplied digital files.

Depending on the project scope, work may include:

- File intake, naming, folder organization, and basic inventory.
- Preparation of scanning lists and capture requirements.
- Coordination with an approved scanning, photography, or reproduction provider.
- Review of file formats, orientation, legibility, resolution, and completeness.
- Separation of preservation-oriented master files from access copies where appropriate.
- Documentation of known gaps, limitations, and source conditions.

KTS does not represent itself as a conservation laboratory, archival repository, copyright office, legal service, or professional records-management authority. Physical conservation, high-volume scanning, specialized photography, and sensitive-material handling may require a qualified partner or vendor.

### Metadata, Location, and Map Organization

KTS organizes approved materials so users can understand what an item is, where it came from, what place it relates to, and how it may be accessed.

This may include:

- Descriptive titles, dates, creators, source references, subjects, locations, and notes.
- File naming conventions and folder structures.
- Geographic coordinates, place names, addresses, routes, boundaries, or map features where supported by the materials.
- Map indexing, georeferencing support, location-linked records, and point-of-interest organization.
- Basic technical and preservation documentation.
- Source, rights, restriction, and public-release fields supplied or approved by the collection steward.
- Exportable spreadsheets, GIS files, manifests, inventories, or metadata tables when included in the scope.

Metadata supports retrieval, management, context, and long-term usability. The Library of Congress identifies sustainable file formats, digital-storage monitoring, content packaging, ingest, and preservation metadata as core parts of digital-preservation practice. [164]

### Public, Staff, and Research Access

KTS can prepare approved materials for the access model selected by the collection steward.

Possible access experiences include:

- Public web maps and digital exhibits.
- Searchable map collections and location-linked records.
- Historic walking trails, heritage points, cemeteries, neighborhoods, businesses, or cultural landscapes.
- Staff-only or researcher-access interfaces.
- Library, museum, visitor-center, storefront, classroom, or event kiosks.
- QR-linked information pages for places, maps, exhibits, buildings, trail stops, or collections.
- Local Wi-Fi landing pages and controlled local-network access.
- Offline-capable access for field, rural, event, continuity, or limited-connectivity settings.
- ALLIS-based access to approved map collections, documents, public information, or organization-specific knowledge.

Public release occurs only after the customer or collection steward reviews and approves the relevant content, access rules, descriptions, maps, and materials.

### Preservation Package and Export

A project may include a documented package for the collection steward to retain independent of a particular KTS-hosted experience.

Depending on the agreement, a preservation package may include:

- Organized project files and agreed digital copies.
- Item inventory and metadata export.
- Map layers, data tables, or location indexes.
- File checksums or integrity documentation where included in scope.
- Folder-structure documentation and naming conventions.
- Access-copy and source-file inventory.
- Notes regarding known restrictions, source limitations, and public-release status.
- Handoff documentation for future staff, stewards, partners, or successors.

KTS can provide backups and integrity checks as a managed service when included in an agreement. The collection steward remains responsible for determining retention requirements, rights, restrictions, and the appropriate long-term custodial arrangement for its materials.

## Project Path

1. **Discovery:** KTS and the collection steward identify the collection, priorities, intended users, rights questions, access goals, risks, budget, and first-phase outcome.
2. **Scope:** KTS prepares a written scope identifying materials, deliverables, roles, pricing, assumptions, approval points, access model, and handoff terms.
3. **Preparation:** KTS inventories, organizes, digitizes or coordinates digitization, prepares metadata, and documents approved materials.
4. **Map and access design:** KTS prepares the agreed archive, map, public experience, staff interface, kiosk, local-network, offline, or ALLIS delivery model.
5. **Review:** The collection steward reviews and approves public-facing content, access settings, descriptions, and materials before release.
6. **Launch or handoff:** KTS delivers the agreed files, map, access experience, training, documentation, and export package.
7. **Managed support, if selected:** KTS provides agreed updates, backups, integrity checks, hosting coordination, technical support, and scheduled export services.

## Stewardship, Rights, and Boundaries

The collection steward retains ownership and authority over its own maps, records, images, documents, data, branding, restrictions, and release decisions unless a written agreement states otherwise.

The collection steward is responsible for:

- Confirming it has the right to provide materials to KTS.
- Identifying copyright, privacy, confidentiality, cultural-sensitivity, donor, contractual, or other restrictions.
- Determining what may be publicly released, restricted, removed, or retained.
- Reviewing and approving public-facing materials before launch.
- Identifying authorized contacts for decisions and approvals.

KTS does not determine copyright ownership, fair use, public-domain status, title, archival retention requirements, cultural-resource eligibility, archaeological sensitivity, or legal access rights. KTS does not provide legal advice, professional archival certification, cultural-resource clearance, archaeology, land surveying, engineering, or regulatory compliance determinations.

Collections involving sensitive, restricted, personally identifiable, archaeological, tribal, sacred, protected, regulated, or safety-critical information require a separate review before KTS accepts the work.

## Pricing Approach

Digital Heritage Map Preservation projects are scoped in phases because collection size, condition, digitization needs, metadata depth, rights review, access model, and required support vary significantly.

Typical pricing approaches include:

| Project phase | Planning range |
|---|---:|
| Collection discovery and preservation assessment | $500–$2,500 |
| Basic organization, inventory, and metadata preparation | $75–$150 per hour or fixed project quote |
| Small map-based access or public heritage pilot | $1,000–$7,500 |
| Larger digitization, archive, kiosk, public-map, or ALLIS implementation | Project-specific proposal |
| Ongoing hosting, updates, backups, and managed support | $150–$1,500 per month, depending on scope |

Third-party scanning, photography, printing, storage, hardware, hosting, travel, platform, and vendor costs are identified separately when applicable.

KTS does not guarantee a grant award, public participation level, visitor traffic, research outcome, historical accuracy beyond the customer-approved sources, legal clearance, or permanent compatibility with third-party platforms.

KTS can contribute:

## Start With One Useful Question

KTS begins with a practical question:

> What local knowledge, maps, records, or stories do you need to preserve—and who needs to be able to find and use them?

The first phase may be as simple as organizing a box of maps, creating a reliable inventory, building a small heritage map, preparing a QR-linked exhibit, or preserving a customer-approved collection for future access. Larger public maps, digital archives, kiosks, and ALLIS-node deployments can follow when the collection and its users are ready.
