## Genealogy and Family-History Preservation

KTS can help families, churches, cemeteries, local-history groups, libraries, and community organizations preserve and organize family-history and genealogy materials.

A genealogy project may include family papers, photographs, letters, obituaries, funeral cards, cemetery records, family Bibles, local maps, deeds, school records, church materials, newspaper clippings, oral-history files, military records, family trees, and location notes.

KTS can help turn an unorganized collection into a documented, searchable, location-aware family-history resource for private family use, approved research access, a reunion, a cemetery project, a church history, or a community archive.

Genealogy often depends on materials such as maps, photographs, newspapers, manuscripts, local histories, and other primary sources. The Library of Congress identifies these types of materials as important resources for family-history research, while the National Archives provides guidance for preserving and digitizing family papers and photographs. [256][261][260]

### Genealogy and Family-History Services

Depending on the agreed scope, KTS may provide:

- Family-archive discovery, inventory, and organization.
- Digitization coordination for approved photographs, papers, letters, clippings, family Bibles, records, and other materials.
- File naming, folder organization, metadata, basic descriptions, and source tracking.
- Private family archive, shared family collection, or approved public-history presentation.
- Location-linked family-history maps showing homes, cemeteries, churches, schools, workplaces, migration routes, military-service locations, or other approved places.
- Cemetery, burial, church, family, or community-record maps.
- Family-reunion maps, QR-linked family-history pages, or accessible digital exhibits.
- Oral-history, photograph, and document organization connected to named people and approved locations.
- Exportable digital-preservation package for family custody and future generations.
- ALLIS-based private, family-controlled, researcher-controlled, public, kiosk, local-network, or offline-capable access where appropriate.

For fragile, unique, bound, oversized, or valuable originals, KTS may recommend a qualified scanning, photography, conservation, library, archive, or records-preservation partner. The National Archives advises careful handling of family papers and photographs during digitization, retaining originals after scanning, adding basic metadata, and maintaining multiple backups. [263]

### Genealogy Project Boundaries

KTS provides information organization, digitization coordination, mapping, preservation support, and approved access tools. KTS does not guarantee genealogical conclusions or provide legal determinations.

Unless specifically contracted with appropriately qualified partners, KTS does not provide:

- Proof of lineage, kinship, inheritance, citizenship, tribal enrollment, or legal identity.
- Legal advice concerning estates, deeds, title, probate, adoption, custody, privacy, copyright, or records access.
- Authentication or forensic analysis of historical documents, photographs, artifacts, handwriting, DNA, or family claims.
- Professional archival appraisal or financial valuation of family materials.
- Access to private genealogy databases, subscription records, restricted archives, DNA accounts, or third-party services not supplied or authorized by the customer.
- Publication of living persons' private information without appropriate authority and approval.

The customer or family steward remains responsible for the accuracy of family relationships, source claims, names, dates, permissions, privacy decisions, and approval of any public release.

### Family Stewardship and Privacy

Genealogy projects may involve living people, sensitive family information, adoption, health information, minor children, disputed relationships, restricted records, family conflict, or culturally sensitive materials.

Before KTS creates a public map, website, QR page, exhibit, or shared family archive, the designated family or collection steward must approve:

- Which individuals, records, photographs, stories, and locations may be shared.
- Whether access is private, password-controlled, family-only, research-only, or public.
- How living persons, minors, sensitive records, and disputed information will be handled.
- Which person or group has authority to make release decisions.
- Any requested removal, correction, restriction, or update process.

KTS can support a private-first approach: organize and preserve materials for the family, then release only the portions the designated steward approves.
