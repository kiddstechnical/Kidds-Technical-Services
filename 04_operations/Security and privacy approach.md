# Security and privacy approach

## Purpose

Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) protects customer, project, collection, and business information by using practical safeguards that match the project scope, data sensitivity, access model, and documented customer requirements.

KTS provides mapping, public-information, digital-preservation, web-connected, QR, kiosk, local-network, offline-capable, and ALLIS-related services. KTS uses security and privacy practices to limit access, protect approved materials, support recovery, and address suspected incidents.

This document establishes an operating and contract baseline. A written project agreement, data schedule, managed-service agreement, or customer security requirement may add project-specific controls. If a written project agreement conflicts with this document, the project agreement controls for that project.

## Scope

This approach applies to information that KTS receives, creates, accesses, stores, processes, maps, organizes, hosts, or delivers while providing KTS services.

Covered information may include:

- Customer contact information, business information, service areas, public locations, and approved public content.
- Maps, GIS files, address lists, routes, points of interest, location records, and map layers.
- Documents, reports, records, photographs, audio, video, oral histories, research files, and digital collections.
- Family-history, genealogy, cemetery, church, archive, museum, library, trail, tourism, and community materials.
- Customer branding, website content, QR-linked information, access instructions, and public-facing materials.
- Metadata, inventories, source records, access restrictions, approval records, and project documentation.
- ALLIS node content, configuration information, support records, and approved access controls.
- KTS business records, project records, invoices, contractor records, and internal operating documentation.

## Security and privacy principles

KTS follows these principles:

- Limit access to the people who need it for assigned work.
- Confirm who has authority to provide materials, approve access, and authorize public release.
- Use the safeguards that fit the project’s actual risks and agreed scope.
- Keep customer materials separate from KTS pre-existing software, methods, templates, and business records.
- Document important access decisions, restrictions, approvals, changes, and known limitations.
- Use customer-approved access models for public, restricted, staff, family, researcher, kiosk, local-network, offline-capable, and ALLIS access.
- Review sensitive, restricted, regulated, safety-critical, and personally identifiable information before accepting the work.
- Prepare for recovery by using backups, exports, documentation, and project closeout procedures when the written scope includes them.
- Address suspected security or privacy incidents promptly and document the response.
- Avoid claims about security controls, certifications, compliance, or recovery capabilities that KTS has not implemented and verified.

## Security responsibility

Carrie Kidd serves as the KTS executive lead and is responsible for assigning security and privacy responsibilities appropriate to KTS operations and individual projects.

KTS may assign defined security, system-administration, hosting, access-management, backup, support, or incident-response tasks to qualified employees, contractors, partner firms, or specialized vendors.

Each person who accesses customer or KTS information must have a documented role and access level. KTS does not give a contributor, contractor, or partner more access than the assigned work requires.

KTS remains responsible for managing its own project process, contributor boundaries, customer communication, and scope control. Customers remain responsible for their own systems, access decisions, funding, legal obligations, source rights, public-release decisions, and internal governance unless a written agreement assigns a defined KTS responsibility.

## Risk review

Before KTS accepts a project, KTS reviews the information type, access model, source restrictions, customer requirements, technical environment, third-party services, and expected delivery method.

KTS may ask the customer or collection steward to identify:

- The type of information included in the project.
- The source and known restrictions for the information.
- The intended users and access model.
- The person authorized to approve access, public release, corrections, and removal.
- Any privacy, confidentiality, donor, grant, contractual, cultural-sensitivity, safety, or legal concerns.
- Any customer security requirements, insurance requirements, or third-party platform requirements.
- Whether the project includes personally identifiable, restricted, regulated, safety-critical, or sensitive information.
- Whether customer materials require a specific storage, access, export, deletion, or retention process.

KTS may limit the scope, require additional written terms, engage a qualified specialist, use customer-managed systems, require a restricted access model, or decline the work when the project risks exceed KTS’s capacity or agreed safeguards.

## Information categories

KTS uses project-specific access and handling decisions. The following categories help KTS identify appropriate project controls.

| Information category | Examples | KTS approach |
|---|---|---|
| Public information | Approved business hours, public maps, visitor information, public trail information, public historic content | KTS may publish after customer or steward approval |
| Internal information | Staff procedures, internal maps, project notes, unpublished collection organization | KTS limits access to authorized customer and KTS contributors |
| Restricted collection information | Restricted records, unpublished research, sensitive site locations, controlled family materials | KTS uses a customer-approved restricted access model and defined contributor access |
| Personal information | Names, contact details, family records, photographs, living-person information, account information | KTS limits access and requires customer or steward approval for use and release |
| Sensitive or regulated information | Health, financial, legal, student, emergency-response, law-enforcement, utility, protected-location, or security information | KTS completes a separate review and may require additional controls, qualified partners, customer-managed systems, or a decision not to accept the work |

KTS does not classify information as public merely because it is old, locally known, found online, supplied by a customer, or included in a historic, genealogy, map, or community collection.

## Identity and access management

KTS controls access through documented roles, user accounts, passwords, authentication settings, system permissions, and customer-approved access models.

KTS uses the following baseline practices where supported by the relevant system and appropriate to the project:

- Use individual accounts instead of shared accounts when practical.
- Use strong, unique passwords for KTS-controlled accounts.
- Use multi-factor authentication for KTS-controlled accounts when the service supports it and the account risk warrants it.
- Limit access to the minimum level needed for assigned work.
- Review access when project roles, contributor assignments, customer contacts, or system needs change.
- Remove or restrict access when a contributor leaves a project or no longer requires access.
- Record access decisions, customer approvals, and known limitations when practical.
- Avoid sending passwords, credentials, restricted files, or access tokens through unapproved or insecure channels.
- Ask customers to identify authorized contacts for access, approval, and account decisions.

KTS does not guarantee that every customer system, third-party service, public platform, partner environment, local network, or customer-managed account supports the same access controls.

## Devices, storage, and software

KTS uses devices, storage locations, software, and third-party services that fit the agreed project scope and available operating capacity.

KTS may use:

- KTS-controlled accounts and storage.
- Customer-controlled accounts, systems, devices, or networks.
- Partner-firm systems under a documented project arrangement.
- Third-party hosting, mapping, storage, communication, web, kiosk, or software services.
- Local-network, kiosk, or offline-capable devices and media.

For KTS-controlled systems, KTS aims to:

- Keep supported software and services updated according to operational needs and available updates.
- Use access controls appropriate to the system and project.
- Maintain an inventory of important project systems, accounts, devices, or services when the project scope requires it.
- Protect devices from unauthorized physical access when KTS controls the device.
- Use backup, export, or recovery practices when a written scope includes them.
- Remove or return customer information from devices and accounts when the project, access requirement, or service agreement requires it.

A customer agreement identifies which party controls domains, hosting, third-party accounts, physical devices, passwords, subscriptions, hardware, and service renewals.

## Backups, exports, and recovery

KTS provides backups, integrity checks, exports, recovery support, and managed storage only when a written scope includes those services.

If a project includes backups or managed access, the agreement identifies:

- The information included in the backup or export.
- The backup, export, or integrity-check frequency.
- The party responsible for storing and protecting copies.
- The party responsible for hosting, account ownership, passwords, devices, and service renewal.
- The customer contact responsible for access and restoration decisions.
- The expected recovery process and support scope.
- The process for ending the service and transferring customer materials.

KTS encourages customers and collection stewards to maintain independent copies of important materials.

KTS does not guarantee permanent preservation, uninterrupted service, recovery from every event, future compatibility with all platforms or file formats, or restoration within a specified time unless a written agreement expressly provides that commitment.

## Contributor and vendor controls

KTS may use employees, contractors, partner firms, and specialized vendors to deliver customer work.

Before a contributor accesses customer materials or systems, KTS defines:

- The assigned work and deliverables.
- The information, systems, accounts, devices, and physical locations needed for the work.
- Confidentiality, privacy, security, and data-handling expectations.
- Customer-contact, public-communication, and approval boundaries.
- Work-product, ownership, and return-or-removal requirements.
- Payment, schedule, quality-review, and closeout expectations.

Contributors must not:

- Use customer information outside the assigned project scope.
- Share customer materials or credentials with unauthorized people.
- Publish, sell, reuse, or create a separate product from customer materials without written authorization.
- Train a separate system on customer materials without written authorization.
- Represent themselves as a customer, collection steward, KTS owner, KTS officer, or authorized spokesperson unless KTS grants that authority in writing.
- Keep customer materials after the assigned work ends unless a written agreement, legal obligation, or KTS business-record requirement requires retention.

KTS removes or limits contributor access when the contributor no longer needs access for assigned work.

## Privacy and public release

The customer or collection steward controls decisions about public release of customer materials.

Before KTS publishes or launches public content, the customer or steward approves the relevant materials, information, access settings, and release conditions.

KTS may ask the customer or steward to approve:

- Public maps, pages, QR links, kiosks, directories, and access instructions.
- Names, descriptions, locations, contact details, photographs, records, and source notes.
- Service areas, business hours, directions, event information, and public notices.
- Family, genealogy, cemetery, archive, heritage, and community information.
- Restricted information, redactions, disclaimers, public-access limits, and removal procedures.
- KTS portfolio, case-study, marketing, donor, sponsor, partner, or public-communication use.

KTS may restrict, correct, remove, or temporarily disable content when:

- The authorized customer or steward requests a change.
- KTS identifies a suspected unauthorized release, material error, technical issue, or security concern.
- A written agreement, legal obligation, platform requirement, or safety concern requires action.

KTS documents the request, action, date, and responsible contact when practical.

## Incident response

KTS treats a suspected security or privacy incident as an event that may involve unauthorized access, loss, disclosure, alteration, destruction, misdirection, or unavailability of customer or KTS information.

If KTS identifies or receives a report of a suspected incident, KTS will take actions appropriate to the scope, available information, and technical environment:

1. **Identify and record the issue.** KTS records the known facts, affected systems or materials, reporter, date, and initial actions.
2. **Contain the issue.** KTS limits access, pauses affected work, disables exposed credentials, restricts publication, or takes other reasonable steps within its control.
3. **Preserve relevant information.** KTS retains relevant project records, access information, logs, messages, files, or screenshots when practical and appropriate.
4. **Assess the impact.** KTS reviews what information, systems, customers, contributors, or public content may be affected.
5. **Notify the appropriate contact.** KTS notifies the designated customer or steward contact according to the project agreement, the known risk, and applicable requirements.
6. **Recover and correct.** KTS restores service, updates access, corrects content, returns to a known working state, or coordinates with the customer, vendor, host, insurer, legal advisor, or qualified specialist as appropriate.
7. **Document and improve.** KTS records the response and identifies reasonable changes to reduce recurrence.

KTS does not guarantee a specific incident-notification period, restoration time, forensic investigation, legal conclusion, regulatory report, or security outcome unless a written agreement expressly states those responsibilities and KTS has accepted them.

## Sensitive and regulated information

KTS completes a separate review before accepting or processing sensitive, regulated, restricted, safety-critical, or personally identifiable information.

This information may include:

- Health, financial, legal, personnel, student, law-enforcement, emergency-response, utility, or protected customer information.
- Information about minors, living family members, adoptions, genetic information, identity documents, or sensitive family records.
- Restricted archaeological, sacred, burial, tribal, cultural, ecological, infrastructure, security, or location information.
- Information subject to contractual confidentiality, donor restrictions, legal holds, regulated-records requirements, or specialized security rules.

KTS may require additional written terms, restricted access controls, customer-managed systems, qualified vendors, legal review, insurance review, or other safeguards before accepting this work.

KTS may decline work when required credentials, licenses, security controls, insurance, staffing, legal review, technical capacity, or customer authority exceed KTS’s documented scope.

## Customer responsibilities

Customers and collection stewards are responsible for:

- Identifying authorized decision-makers and access contacts.
- Confirming they have the right to provide materials and authorize KTS to use them.
- Identifying known restrictions, privacy concerns, confidentiality requirements, and public-release limits.
- Reviewing public content, access settings, maps, customer information, and project deliverables before release.
- Maintaining their own required legal, grant, donor, records-management, privacy, security, and regulatory obligations.
- Maintaining independent copies of important materials unless a written managed-service agreement assigns that responsibility to KTS.
- Managing customer-controlled accounts, domains, hosting, passwords, devices, networks, and subscriptions unless a written agreement assigns KTS a defined responsibility.
- Promptly reporting suspected unauthorized access, errors, lost devices, inaccurate public information, changed restrictions, or security concerns that may affect the project.

## Limits

This document describes KTS operating practices. It does not constitute legal, privacy, cybersecurity, insurance, records-management, regulatory, tax, or compliance advice.

KTS does not claim that it is:

- Certified, audited, breach-proof, penetration-tested, or secure against every threat.
- Compliant with HIPAA, FERPA, CJIS, PCI DSS, GDPR, NIST, SOC 2, the FTC Safeguards Rule, or another legal, regulatory, contractual, or industry framework.
- Authorized to process every category of regulated, sensitive, restricted, or safety-critical information.
- Responsible for the security, privacy, availability, or behavior of every customer system, public platform, third-party vendor, internet connection, hardware device, or partner environment.
- Able to guarantee encryption, multi-factor authentication, logging, backup immutability, monitoring, restoration, uptime, data residency, or notification timeframes unless a written project agreement identifies the specific control and KTS has implemented it.

KTS uses risk-based safeguards within the agreed project scope. If a project requires formal security assurance, regulatory certification, specialized technical controls, professional insurance, legal review, independent assessment, or regulated-data expertise, KTS may work with qualified specialists or decline the work.

## Review and updates

KTS reviews this approach when KTS changes its services, staffing, technology, hosting, access practices, insurance, customer requirements, or risk profile.

KTS may update this document to reflect operational changes. A written project agreement controls the security and privacy terms for the applicable customer project.

## Operating principle

KTS follows a practical security and privacy rule:

> KTS limits access, documents authority, applies safeguards appropriate to the project, prepares for recovery within the agreed scope, and responds promptly to suspected incidents.

KTS does not present practical safeguards as a formal cybersecurity assurance. Like a mapping organization that provides planning and public-information maps without claiming to provide a licensed boundary survey, KTS works within its documented technical scope and engages qualified specialists when a project requires a higher level of security, regulatory, legal, or professional assurance.
