# Data rights and collection stewardship

## Purpose

Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) provides technical services that help customers and collection stewards organize, preserve, map, access, and share approved materials.

This policy establishes the baseline for how KTS handles customer-provided maps, records, photographs, documents, data, media, branding, and related materials. It applies to KTS mapping, digital-preservation, genealogy, public-information, website-connected, QR, kiosk, local-network, offline-capable, and ALLIS-related services.

A project agreement, statement of work, service agreement, data schedule, or other written project document may add requirements that apply to a specific customer, collection, funding source, partner, or deployment. If a project agreement conflicts with this policy, the project agreement controls for that project.

## Scope

This policy applies to materials that a customer, collection steward, partner, or authorized contributor provides to KTS or authorizes KTS to access.

Covered materials may include:

- Maps, plats, GIS files, geospatial data, address lists, routes, boundaries, and location records.
- Documents, reports, letters, records, clippings, research files, and scanned materials.
- Photographs, audio, video, oral histories, images, and digital media.
- Archive, library, museum, historic, cemetery, church, family-history, genealogy, trail, tourism, and community collections.
- Business information, service areas, hours, locations, menus, services, customer instructions, and public contact information.
- Customer logos, branding, websites, public text, and approved public-facing content.
- Metadata, inventory records, descriptions, source information, access restrictions, and release instructions.
- Content imported into an ALLIS node or another KTS-managed access environment.

This policy does not transfer legal ownership of materials to KTS.

## Terms

### Customer

A person, business, organization, government entity, family representative, project lead, or authorized party that contracts with KTS or authorizes KTS to provide services.

### Collection steward

The person or organization authorized to make decisions about a collection, including access, restrictions, public release, corrections, removal, retention, and use of collection materials.

A customer may also be the collection steward. If the customer and collection steward are different, KTS follows the authority identified in the written project agreement.

### Customer materials

Maps, records, data, photographs, documents, media, branding, and other materials that a customer or collection steward provides to KTS or authorizes KTS to access.

### Project deliverables

The work that KTS creates for a customer under a written scope. Deliverables may include maps, data files, metadata, inventories, web pages, QR tools, public-information pages, access copies, preservation packages, training materials, exports, reports, or configured access experiences.

### KTS materials

Pre-existing KTS intellectual property, including ALLIS, KTS software, code, methods, templates, workflows, technical documentation, branding, configuration practices, and business processes.

### ALLIS node

A configured Artificial Learning & Location Intelligence System environment that organizes and provides access to approved materials, maps, public information, organizational knowledge, or collections through an agreed access model.

### Public release

Making material available to the general public through a website, map, QR code, public kiosk, public event, public exhibit, public directory, public network, public communication, or another unrestricted access method.

## Stewardship principles

KTS uses the following stewardship principles:

- The collection steward controls decisions about customer materials, access, restrictions, corrections, and public release.
- KTS uses customer materials only for the agreed project purpose and scope.
- KTS gives contributors only the access they need to complete assigned work.
- KTS documents important assumptions, known restrictions, source limitations, and customer approvals.
- KTS seeks approval before publicly releasing customer materials or creating a public access experience.
- KTS provides agreed exports, documentation, and handoff materials at project closeout.
- KTS separates customer materials from KTS pre-existing platform, software, methods, templates, and business materials.
- KTS does not claim ownership of customer materials unless a written agreement expressly states otherwise.
- KTS does not make legal determinations about ownership, copyright, public-domain status, fair use, privacy, title, inheritance, cultural sensitivity, archival retention, or regulatory obligations.

## Customer and steward responsibilities

Before KTS uses customer materials, the customer or collection steward must identify the person or organization authorized to provide materials and make project decisions.

The customer or collection steward is responsible for:

- Providing materials that it has the right to provide to KTS.
- Identifying known restrictions, privacy concerns, confidentiality requirements, donor conditions, contract restrictions, cultural-sensitivity concerns, and public-release limits.
- Identifying the authorized decision-maker for access, corrections, removal, public release, and final acceptance.
- Reviewing and approving public-facing materials before launch or release.
- Providing accurate source information, business details, locations, descriptions, contact information, and access instructions.
- Informing KTS when material changes, restrictions change, or public information becomes inaccurate.
- Maintaining any legal, donor, grant, records-management, or institutional approvals required for its own materials.

If the customer or steward cannot confirm authority, rights, restrictions, or release approval, KTS may limit access, use a restricted project workflow, defer the work, or decline the work.

## KTS rights and responsibilities

KTS may use customer materials only as needed to perform the agreed services.

KTS may:

- Receive, organize, inventory, name, classify, index, geocode, map, convert, process, and prepare customer materials.
- Create metadata, access copies, map layers, indexes, descriptions, public-information pages, QR tools, data exports, preservation packages, and other agreed project deliverables.
- Configure customer-approved materials for public, restricted, staff, family, researcher, kiosk, local-network, offline-capable, or ALLIS access.
- Create backups, integrity records, project documentation, and technical logs when included in the project scope.
- Engage qualified contributors, contractors, partner firms, or vendors to complete assigned work under appropriate confidentiality, access, and project terms.
- Retain limited internal project records needed for invoicing, accounting, support, quality control, legal obligations, dispute resolution, and business operations.

KTS will:

- Use reasonable administrative and technical practices appropriate to the agreed project scope.
- Limit contributor access to the materials and systems required for assigned work.
- Follow the customer’s documented access and release instructions.
- Seek customer or steward approval before public release, unless the project agreement identifies materials that the customer has already approved for public use.
- Provide agreed deliverables, exports, handoff materials, and access information at closeout.
- Remove or limit contributor access when a contributor no longer needs access for assigned work.
- Document known limitations when source materials are incomplete, unclear, damaged, inconsistent, or restricted.

KTS does not guarantee that all customer materials are accurate, complete, legally cleared, historically verified, permanently preserved, free from technical error, or suitable for public release.

## Rights in customer materials

The customer or collection steward retains ownership of its customer materials unless a written agreement states otherwise.

KTS does not receive ownership of customer materials merely because KTS:

- Receives or stores the materials.
- Organizes, scans, indexes, maps, geocodes, converts, or describes the materials.
- Creates metadata, access copies, or project documentation.
- Hosts, displays, or provides access to materials through a website, kiosk, local network, QR tool, or ALLIS node.
- Creates a backup, export, preservation package, or technical copy.
- Supports a public, staff, researcher, family, or community access experience.

The customer grants KTS a limited, nonexclusive right to use customer materials only as necessary to perform the agreed project services.

If a customer wants KTS to use a project deliverable as a public demonstration, portfolio example, case study, training example, or marketing material, KTS will obtain the appropriate permission before use.

## Rights in KTS materials and ALLIS

KTS retains ownership of KTS materials, including ALLIS, KTS software, code, methods, templates, workflows, technical documentation, branding, pre-existing map designs, configuration practices, and business processes.

A customer does not receive ownership of KTS materials merely because the customer:

- Purchases a KTS service.
- Funds a project.
- Uses an ALLIS node.
- Hosts, tests, promotes, or contributes materials to a KTS-supported project.
- Participates in an H4H, MountainShares, ALLIS, partner-firm, grant-funded, donor-funded, or community-benefit project.

A written agreement may grant the customer a license or specified use rights for KTS materials that are included in a project deliverable. The agreement must identify the scope, duration, territory, transfer rights, modification rights, support expectations, and termination terms of that license.

## Project deliverables

The written project scope identifies the deliverables that KTS will provide.

Deliverables may include:

- Print-ready, web-ready, or interactive maps.
- GIS files, data tables, address lists, geocoded data, and map layers.
- Collection inventories, metadata, source lists, naming conventions, and folder structures.
- Digitized or organized access copies of approved materials.
- Preservation packages, data exports, project records, and handoff documentation.
- Website pages, QR-linked information tools, map embeds, kiosk content, and public-information tools.
- ALLIS configuration, access instructions, training materials, and project-specific technical documentation.
- Managed-service reports, backup records, update records, and agreed support materials.

Unless a written agreement states otherwise, KTS provides the customer with the agreed project deliverables after the customer meets applicable payment obligations.

KTS may retain internal working files, templates, technical notes, configuration records, quality-control records, and business records. KTS will not use retained internal records to create a new public use of customer materials without authorization.

## Access models

KTS and the customer or collection steward select an access model for each project.

| Access model | Intended users | Approval requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Public access | General public | Customer or steward approval before release |
| Restricted access | Approved users with defined limits | Customer or steward defines access rules |
| Staff access | Designated staff, employees, or authorized workers | Customer identifies authorized users |
| Member access | Approved members, subscribers, or participants | Customer defines membership and access process |
| Family access | Family members or designated representatives | Family steward defines access and sharing rules |
| Researcher access | Approved researchers or project participants | Collection steward defines review and access process |
| Kiosk access | Users at an approved physical site | Customer approves site, content, and public instructions |
| Local-network access | Users on an approved local network or Wi-Fi environment | Customer approves network access and site conditions |
| Offline-capable access | Users with approved local files, devices, or field access | Customer approves distribution, device, and update process |

The written project scope identifies:

- The selected access model.
- The person or organization authorized to approve access.
- The materials included in the access experience.
- Any restrictions, exclusions, or expiration dates.
- The process for adding, changing, removing, or correcting materials.
- The customer contact responsible for access decisions.

KTS does not change public or restricted access settings without customer or steward authorization, except when KTS must take temporary action to protect security, prevent unauthorized access, address a technical incident, or comply with applicable law.

## Intake and source review

Before KTS begins work with a collection or data set, KTS may request information about:

- Collection or data description.
- Source, creator, date, format, condition, and location.
- Known ownership, rights, restrictions, permissions, and release conditions.
- Intended users and access model.
- Customer contact and authorized steward.
- Required metadata, naming, format, export, or preservation requirements.
- Privacy, confidentiality, cultural-sensitivity, safety, or regulated-data concerns.
- Existing systems, storage locations, hosting arrangements, and access controls.
- Required approvals before public release.

KTS may document a source inventory, intake record, transfer record, access schedule, project data map, or other record that identifies materials received and the agreed handling instructions.

## Contributor access and confidentiality

KTS may assign customer work to employees, contractors, partner firms, or specialized vendors.

Before a contributor accesses customer materials, KTS will define the contributor’s assigned role, scope, access level, confidentiality duties, data-handling requirements, work-product terms, and customer-contact boundaries.

KTS limits contributor access to the information and systems required for the assigned work.

Contributors must not:

- Use customer materials outside the assigned project scope.
- Share customer materials with unauthorized people.
- Publish, sell, reuse, train a separate system on, or create a separate product from customer materials without written authorization.
- Represent themselves as the customer, collection steward, KTS owner, KTS officer, or authorized public spokesperson unless KTS authorizes that role in writing.
- Retain customer materials after the assigned work ends unless the agreement or KTS records-retention requirement requires retention.

When a contributor no longer needs access, KTS will remove or limit the contributor’s access according to the project requirements and available technical controls.

## Processing and derivative outputs

KTS may create project outputs from customer materials as needed to perform the agreed services.

Project outputs may include:

- Metadata, inventories, indexes, captions, descriptions, and source notes.
- Geocoded records, map layers, points of interest, routes, and location indexes.
- Access copies, thumbnails, web-ready files, print-ready files, and export files.
- Organized folders, file names, preservation packages, and documentation.
- Search indexes, retrieval tools, knowledge organization, and approved ALLIS configuration.
- Public maps, staff maps, family maps, visitor maps, kiosks, QR tools, and website-connected access experiences.

The customer or steward approves public release of derivative outputs when those outputs include customer materials, customer information, restricted collection information, family information, or customer branding.

KTS may use generalized technical knowledge, non-customer-specific methods, and KTS pre-existing templates to improve its own operations. KTS will not disclose or reuse customer-specific confidential materials outside the agreed project scope.

## Public release and communications

KTS releases customer materials publicly only when the customer or collection steward authorizes the release.

Before public release, KTS may ask the customer or steward to approve:

- The list of included materials.
- Titles, descriptions, metadata, source notes, and location information.
- Public maps, QR codes, web pages, kiosk screens, search results, and access instructions.
- Access limitations, restricted materials, redactions, disclaimers, and contact information.
- Use of names, logos, photographs, testimonials, and project descriptions.
- Portfolio, case-study, marketing, donor, sponsor, partner, or public-communication use.

KTS may remove, restrict, correct, or temporarily disable public materials when:

- The customer or authorized steward requests a change.
- KTS identifies an apparent unauthorized release, technical issue, security issue, or material error.
- A project agreement, legal obligation, platform requirement, or safety concern requires action.

KTS will document the request, action, date, and responsible contact when practical.

## Storage, backups, and managed service

KTS provides storage, backups, integrity checks, hosting coordination, and managed access only when a written scope includes those services.

The project agreement identifies:

- Whether KTS stores customer materials.
- Where and how long KTS stores materials, when this information is appropriate to provide.
- Whether KTS creates backups, integrity records, access copies, or exports.
- Who manages hosting, domain names, third-party accounts, hardware, passwords, and physical devices.
- The customer contact responsible for access and update decisions.
- The process for maintenance, updates, restoration, support, and service termination.
- The customer’s responsibility to maintain independent copies of important materials.

KTS does not guarantee permanent preservation, uninterrupted availability, disaster-proof storage, recovery from every technical event, or future compatibility with every device, platform, format, or third-party service.

## Export, handoff, and closeout

At project closeout, KTS provides the exports, files, documentation, and handoff materials identified in the written agreement.

A closeout package may include:

- Agreed maps, data files, metadata, inventories, and reports.
- Approved access copies and project deliverables.
- Folder-structure documentation, naming conventions, and source inventories.
- Exported customer data and project-specific configuration information.
- User instructions, training materials, access information, and support contacts.
- Backup, update, hosting, and maintenance information when included in the scope.
- Known limitations, restrictions, assumptions, and unresolved items.

The customer remains responsible for downloading, storing, maintaining, and protecting its own copies after handoff unless a managed-service agreement assigns those responsibilities to KTS.

## Corrections, removals, and access changes

The customer or collection steward may request a correction, removal, restriction, access change, or update for materials within the project scope.

A request should identify:

- The material or location that requires a change.
- The requested action.
- The reason for the request.
- The authorized person making the request.
- Any deadline, safety concern, privacy concern, or legal requirement.

KTS will review requests according to the project agreement, access model, technical feasibility, customer authority, and available support arrangement.

If a request falls outside the agreed scope or managed-service plan, KTS may provide a separate estimate before completing the work.

## Sensitive and restricted information

KTS completes a separate review before accepting or processing sensitive, restricted, regulated, safety-critical, or personally identifiable information.

This category may include:

- Health, education, financial, legal, personnel, law-enforcement, emergency-response, utility, or protected customer information.
- Information about minors, living family members, adoptions, genetic information, identity documents, or sensitive family records.
- Restricted archaeological, sacred, burial, tribal, cultural, ecological, infrastructure, security, or location information.
- Information subject to contractual confidentiality, donor restrictions, legal holds, regulated-records requirements, or specialized security rules.

KTS may require additional written terms, restricted access controls, qualified vendors, customer-managed systems, legal review, insurance review, or other safeguards before accepting this work.

KTS may decline work when the requested safeguards, authority, credentials, insurance, security controls, or legal requirements exceed KTS’s scope or capacity.

## Limitations

This policy is an operational and contractual baseline. It is not legal, tax, archival, privacy, cybersecurity, intellectual-property, records-management, grant-management, or regulatory advice.

KTS does not determine:

- Legal ownership, copyright, public-domain status, fair use, trademark rights, or licensing rights.
- Title, deed, boundary, inheritance, estate, citizenship, tribal-enrollment, or family-relationship claims.
- Legal privacy obligations, public-records obligations, records-retention requirements, or regulatory requirements.
- Cultural-resource eligibility, archaeological clearance, cultural sensitivity, or public-release eligibility.
- Archival certification, conservation treatment, long-term repository suitability, or permanent preservation status.
- Compliance with a law, regulation, standard, contract, grant condition, or donor requirement unless a written agreement specifically assigns a defined task and KTS is qualified to perform it.

Customers, collection stewards, and project partners should obtain qualified legal, archival, privacy, security, records-management, insurance, tax, grant, and other professional advice when their project requires it.

## Operating principle

KTS follows a simple stewardship rule:

> The collection steward controls the materials and release decisions. KTS provides the technical work needed to organize, preserve, map, access, and export approved materials under documented project terms.

KTS protects this boundary by documenting authority, limiting access, confirming public release, separating customer materials from KTS materials, providing agreed exports, and using written project terms for each engagement.
