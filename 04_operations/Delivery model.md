# Delivery model

## Purpose

Kidd's Technical Services, LLC (KTS) is a founder-led technical-services organization. KTS delivers mapping, local-information, digital-preservation, web-connected, and ALLIS-related services through qualified paid staff, contractors, partner firms, and specialized vendors.

Carrie Kidd leads KTS. She directs customer relationships, project decisions, information governance, quality review, and final delivery. KTS builds the staffing and operating capacity needed to deliver customer work without requiring Carrie to complete every production task.

KTS uses customer revenue, project budgets, appropriate financing, partnership work, and other lawful capital sources to pay contributors and operate the business.

## Delivery commitment

KTS delivers work through a documented process:

```text
Customer, partner, project steward, or community organization identifies a need
        ↓
KTS confirms the need, project authority, scope, funding, and delivery requirements
        ↓
The customer or authorized project pathway approves the work
        ↓
KTS assigns qualified staff, contractors, partner firms, or vendors
        ↓
KTS prepares and delivers the agreed maps, records, tools, access experience, or managed service
        ↓
The customer or steward reviews and approves applicable content and access settings
        ↓
KTS launches, hands off, exports, trains, or begins managed service
        ↓
KTS records acceptance, payment, support obligations, and follow-up opportunities
```

KTS manages the delivery process. The customer or project steward retains authority over its own materials, funding decisions, public-release decisions, branding, and access rules.

## Customer and steward authority

The customer, collection steward, or authorized project lead controls its own approved materials, branding, public information, access rules, and funding decisions.

KTS provides technical work within the agreed scope. KTS does not own customer-provided maps, records, photographs, documents, data, logos, or other materials unless a written agreement states otherwise.

Before KTS publicly releases or launches project materials, the customer or steward approves:

- Public maps, text, photographs, records, links, and information.
- Access settings for public, staff, member, researcher, family, kiosk, local-network, or offline use.
- Business details, including locations, service areas, hours, directions, contact information, and public instructions.
- Historic, genealogy, family, collection, or community information that the customer wants to release.
- Use of customer materials in a KTS portfolio, case study, demonstration, or public communication.

## Project process

### 1. Confirm the project

KTS confirms the customer need, intended users, desired result, decision-maker, source materials, access requirements, timeline, budget, funding pathway, and project risks.

If authority, funding, rights, privacy, security, scope, access, procurement, licensing, insurance, or technical feasibility is unclear, KTS may defer or decline the work.

### 2. Define the scope

KTS prepares a proposal, statement of work, service agreement, subcontract, or other written document appropriate to the engagement.

The written scope identifies:

- Customer, project steward, lead organization, and authorized decision-maker.
- Deliverables, schedule, assumptions, exclusions, and review points.
- Price, deposits, milestone payments, or managed-service charges.
- Customer responsibilities, source materials, approvals, and required access.
- Ownership, licensing, confidentiality, public-release, and data-export terms.
- Support, maintenance, hosting, handoff, and termination expectations.
- Relevant partner, H4H, MountainShares, ALLIS, grant, donor, sponsor, or program requirements.

### 3. Activate the project

KTS starts production after the customer or project pathway provides the required authorization, funding confirmation, payment arrangement, source materials, and access.

Depending on the project, KTS may require:

- Full payment before delivery for a small defined project.
- A deposit before work begins.
- Milestone payments for a larger project.
- Written confirmation that a grant, sponsor, donor, public entity, nonprofit, or partner firm has authorized the work.
- A separate budget before KTS hires a contractor or specialized vendor.

### 4. Assign the delivery team

KTS identifies the required skills and assigns qualified employees, contractors, partner firms, or vendors.

KTS provides each contributor only the customer information, system access, and physical access necessary to complete the assigned work. KTS documents the contributor’s role, deliverables, payment terms, schedule, confidentiality duties, data-handling requirements, customer-contact boundaries, and quality-review process.

### 5. Produce and review work

KTS completes the agreed work. Project work may include:

- Mapping, GIS data preparation, geocoding, and location organization.
- Public-information, visitor, trail, service-area, event, venue, and resource maps.
- Website-connected maps, QR hubs, landing pages, kiosks, and customer-access tools.
- Collection inventory, digitization coordination, file organization, metadata, preservation packages, and map-based archive access.
- ALLIS discovery, configuration, access design, deployment, hosting coordination, and support.
- Training, documentation, data export, handoff, and managed-service preparation.

Carrie Kidd or an assigned qualified reviewer checks work against the agreed scope, customer-approved sources, usability requirements, access boundaries, branding requirements, and documentation requirements.

### 6. Obtain customer approval

KTS gives the customer, collection steward, partner firm, or authorized project representative an opportunity to review deliverables before public release, launch, or closeout.

The customer reviews information that depends on its authority, including:

- Names, locations, service areas, business hours, directions, and contact information.
- Historic claims, family information, collection descriptions, and source information.
- Public-access settings, restricted materials, and privacy decisions.
- Brand use, public instructions, and release decisions.

KTS records approvals, requested revisions, known limitations, and final acceptance under the project agreement.

### 7. Launch, hand off, or begin managed service

KTS may complete a project by:

- Delivering maps, files, print-ready materials, web pages, QR tools, data layers, or preservation packages.
- Launching a public, staff, family, researcher, kiosk, local-network, offline-capable, or ALLIS access experience.
- Training designated customer contacts.
- Delivering project documentation, data exports, backups, or a preservation package.
- Beginning a defined care plan or managed-service agreement.

KTS provides future updates, custom work, hosting, technical support, and maintenance only when a written agreement includes those services.

### 8. Close the project

At closeout, KTS records:

- Final deliverables and customer acceptance.
- Completed payment and remaining obligations.
- Source files, exports, access credentials, documentation, and handoff materials.
- Support, hosting, maintenance, update, backup, and export arrangements.
- Customer feedback, testimonial, referral, or portfolio permission when appropriate.
- Opportunities for follow-on work or recurring support.

## Managed service

KTS may provide managed service after deployment. A written agreement defines the included services.

A managed-service agreement may include:

- Hosting or hosting coordination.
- Routine software and configuration updates.
- Backups and integrity checks.
- Approved updates to content, maps, collections, and public information.
- Support for designated customer contacts.
- Customer training and onboarding support.
- Monitoring of agreed technical components.
- Periodic access, governance, and export review.
- Incident communication and recovery support within the agreed scope.

A managed-service agreement does not include unlimited custom development, unlimited updates, emergency response, 24-hour support, cybersecurity certification, legal certification, regulated-data management, third-party vendor charges, or work outside the defined service plan.

## Funding and workforce capacity

KTS needs sufficient revenue and capital to fund qualified people, tools, insurance, systems, and operating capacity.

KTS may fund delivery capacity through:

- Customer payments, deposits, and milestone payments.
- Recurring managed-service revenue.
- Paid subcontract and partner-firm work.
- Authorized customer grant, sponsor, donor, public, or community-benefit project budgets.
- Founder investment and retained earnings.
- Appropriate commercial financing, working-capital financing, equipment financing, or other lawful business capital.
- H4H-sponsored community-benefit projects that have documented approval, funding, scope, and payment terms.
- MountainShares or ALLIS projects that have documented governance, authority, funding, scope, and delivery terms.

KTS evaluates each hiring or contractor decision against project budget, payment schedule, worker classification, payroll or contractor-payment requirements, insurance, supervision, confidentiality, customer-access needs, and expected cash flow.

KTS aims to create reliable paid technical work. It does not rely on unpaid labor or require the founder to carry production work indefinitely.

## Delivery through partners

KTS may deliver services through direct customer engagements, partner-firm relationships, H4H community-benefit projects, and approved MountainShares or ALLIS pathways.

### Direct KTS engagement

The customer contracts with and pays KTS directly. KTS manages the work and assigns employees, contractors, partner firms, or vendors as needed.

### Partner-firm engagement

A website, marketing, design, IT, preservation, planning, consulting, or other firm may engage KTS as a subcontractor, co-delivery provider, white-label contributor, or direct vendor to a shared customer.

The written agreement identifies communication, scope, branding, payment, ownership, support, and approval responsibilities.

### H4H community-benefit engagement

If H4H accepts a qualifying community-benefit project, H4H manages charitable purpose, project governance, funding stewardship, beneficiary focus, and authorized approvals.

KTS may provide technical implementation through a separate written scope and approved payment arrangement. H4H does not automatically select KTS, and KTS does not control H4H charitable funds, donor decisions, grant decisions, or project governance.

### MountainShares and ALLIS engagement

If a project uses an approved MountainShares or ALLIS pathway, the relevant program rules, governance process, access terms, project agreement, funding source, and customer or steward authority govern the work.

KTS provides technical implementation, mapping, preservation, access tools, support, or managed service only under a documented scope with defined ownership, access, funding, stewardship, maintenance, and export terms.

## Service boundaries

KTS provides technical implementation, mapping, information organization, preservation support, public-access tools, and related managed services.

Unless KTS separately contracts with an appropriately qualified provider, KTS does not provide:

- Land surveying, boundary determination, title research, legal property opinions, or legal advice.
- Engineering design, construction certification, safety certification, or professional engineering services.
- Emergency dispatch, emergency-command systems, real-time public-safety operations, or emergency response.
- Medical, legal, financial, insurance, tax, or regulatory advice.
- Guaranteed search rankings, grant awards, funding approvals, visitor traffic, business revenue, or third-party platform outcomes.
- Professional archival certification, conservation treatment, cultural-resource clearance, archaeological services, or records-retention certification.
- Cybersecurity, accessibility, privacy, or regulatory certification.
- Work that requires credentials, licenses, security clearances, insurance, or professional authorization that KTS or its assigned contributor does not hold.

KTS completes a separate review before accepting work involving sensitive, regulated, restricted, safety-critical, or personally identifiable information.

## Operating principle

KTS delivers through accountable leadership and qualified paid capacity.

> Carrie Kidd leads the organization, protects customer authority, directs project delivery, and maintains quality. KTS builds the team, systems, revenue, and funding necessary for qualified people to perform the work.

KTS builds a durable organization that serves customers reliably, pays contributors for defined work, protects customer materials and local knowledge, and expands from practical entry services into larger mapping, preservation, managed-service, partnership, and ALLIS deployments.
